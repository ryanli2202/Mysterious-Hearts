## The story's centre

At twenty, Mavis successfully assassinated the previous Ace of Spades while working for another group. The Syndicate subsequently recruited her. About five years later, someone from that former life has acquired the means to exploit the killing, her recruitment and the methods she has since developed. The Crow orders her to close the breach. She wants the operation ended for a more personal reason: her former handler still talks about her as something that belongs to him.

The emotional arc is from relying only on herself to accepting chosen, specific reliance on the other Aces. Her loyalty to the Crow remains absolute in the present. The unsettling question is how she came to experience service to him as a choice worth defending. The story can show her sincere conviction and the Crow's exploitative ambition together, without turning him into a benevolent rescuer.

Use close third-person Mavis viewpoint. Her spoken lines remain spare, practical and dry; her interiority can be richer. Let her notice a repaired cable, a chair placed too close, the sound that disappears when a door closes. Her established interest in field recording becomes a recurring personal detail, not a supernatural forensic power.

## Antagonist and causal chain

**Proposed former group:** a compact contract network that used Mavis for specialist assignments. The Broker assigned the predecessor assassination to remove an obstacle to its business. Mavis knew she was killing a Syndicate operative; the full value of the Ace seat can become clear in the recruitment sequence. Whether she already knew the title is a story-lock decision.

**Why the Crow recruits her:** the death creates a vacancy and demonstrates exceptional ability. He values the person capable of removing his specialist more than an act of sentimental revenge. He offers purpose, resources and operational latitude in direct service, expecting complete loyalty in return. This is a calculated acquisition by an ambitious ruler. Mavis's acceptance must be shown across choices, missions and consequences rather than explained by a single persuasive speech.

**Why the threat returns now:** a surviving archive custodian has recently offered the Broker original dispatch records, letting him substantiate a history he could previously only claim. He combines those records with newly gathered observations of current assignments. The archive concerns contracts and operational links; it does not reveal Mavis's legal identity or the Crow's biography. Historical memory can identify a lead, but present corroboration proves who currently controls it.

**What the Broker wants:** leverage over Mavis and access to Syndicate operations, with a sale of authenticated operational material as the fallback. He frames her replacement of the prior Spade as evidence that people are interchangeable tools and threatens to put another instrument in her place. His scheme is dangerous because of timing, access and competing objectives, not because he is inexplicably more powerful than the entire Syndicate.

**What changes:** the first lead suggests someone is merely copying an old technique. Independent records reveal acquisition of the archive; a live mission establishes its use; a compromised negotiation identifies the Broker's sale. The final operation closes a specific transfer network, with any unrecovered copies recorded as a bounded cost. It does not magically erase every historical record worldwide.

## Choice and consequence design

Proposed dimensions are discrete recorded decisions, not a new morality meter:

- **Exposure:** selected approach and actual mission result affect who must cover the extraction and what the enemy can prepare. Show the consequence; avoid an invisible accumulating penalty.
- **Evidence:** authenticated records, unsupported suspicions and missing material remain distinct. A sound recording may suggest a lead; another observation must corroborate its significance.
- **Reliance:** agree a specific role with a colleague, supply the needed information and decide whether to trust that preparation under pressure. Optional quiet scenes enrich the payoff but cannot hide a required ability unlock.
- **Disclosure:** choose whether to share a personal detail privately; log recipients and topic. Withholding personal memories does not withhold the basic operational information required for a viable route.
- **Final priority:** where both plans are feasible, choose the costs of securing the transfer and defeating the handler. Display the known tradeoff before commitment and honour the real combat result.

The player cannot reverse the prior Spade's established death, invent an undisclosed birth identity, automatically defect, command Sylvanis or defeat him as a relationship check. Mavis retains practical agency within her assigned objective. A major future decision to revise her absolute loyalty would be a separate change to current canon.

### Suggested climax structure and endings

**Phase one — Entry:** the preparations from MV-06 and MV-09–10 determine available cover and support. Infiltration has an objective beyond reaching a boss: identify and contain the authenticated transfer.

**Phase two — Separation:** the Broker's countermeasure divides the objectives already established in the briefing. The player sees what Mavis can personally reach and what a teammate has committed to cover. Avoid an unexplained hostage or a new countdown introduced only to force a choice.

**Phase three — Confrontation:** resolve the prepared protection and Broker encounter using supported mission/combat rules. Mavis faces a credible opponent and situation, not a mandatory fight against the Crow or Sylvanis. Their power cannot substitute for her agency or provide an automatic rescue.

Proposed resolved outcomes, all with a full epilogue:

| Outcome | Observable condition to define in implementation | Emotional and world result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The file closed** | Broker's operation resolved; authenticated transfer contained; agreed support objectives completed | She ends the handler's practical leverage and acknowledges the other Aces' contribution. The Crow gains a limited, named strategic advantage. |
| **A costly success** | Core hostile operation resolved, with a recorded exposure, lost asset or incomplete secondary retrieval | The personal conflict closes, but the debrief names the specific cost and required follow-up. Do not claim total secrecy or invent unseen copies. |
| **Withdrawal / retry** | The core mission is not resolved | Treat as an unfinished encounter branch with a truthful recovery/checkpoint, not a premature campaign completion. Do not replay earlier rewards or erase recorded consequences. |

For the recommended first manuscript, the Broker's permanent removal is part of the successful authorised assignment; final name, combat presentation and exact resolution are still to be locked. A live-capture ending would require an explicit order, custody support and its own aftermath, so it is optional scope rather than a promised extra branch. No ending transfers the Spade title to the antagonist or destroys the Syndicate.
