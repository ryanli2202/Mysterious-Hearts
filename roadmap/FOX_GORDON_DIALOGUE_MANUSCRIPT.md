## Chapter 1 — The street before the file

### FD01 — Before opening [FG01]

**Fox viewpoint:** Fox noticed the shopkeeper checking the street before turning the sign. He knew the difference between looking for customers and waiting for somebody one did not want to see. It was a useful observation, not yet an explanation. He kept the thought to himself until the person chose to speak.

**Gordon viewpoint:** Gordon had questions prepared. He put them aside long enough to ask whether this was still a good time. A person agreeing yesterday did not become obliged to be ready today, particularly with two investigators standing in the doorway.

**Shared scene:**

“Do you want to do this here?” Gordon asked.

The shopkeeper looked toward the counter. “I need to open.”

“We can keep out of the way,” Fox said.

“You say that. People notice you.”

Fox glanced toward Gordon. “Mostly him.”

The shopkeeper almost smiled, then did not.

Gordon moved away from the centre of the entrance. “We can arrange another time. You decide whether to give an account.”

“I want it stopped. I don't want everyone deciding I've started something.”

“Tell us what actually happened,” Gordon said. “We won't ask you to answer for the whole street.”

The shopkeeper took out the three demands, but kept a hand on them.

“And tomorrow?”

Neither man answered immediately.

“Tomorrow still matters,” Fox said.

“That isn't a promise.”

“No.” He looked at the papers. “It's why we're asking about the contact route as well as the collector.”

The shopkeeper let go of the documents one at a time. The dates were supplied willingly. Gordon checked how each copy had been retained, and Fox listened without turning every uncertain phrase into a reason to interrupt.

### FD02 — The same doorway [FG02; trigger and return]

At the back of the shop, Gordon stopped short of the threshold.

“Street side?” he asked Fox.

Fox looked past him. “If you're staying with the account.”

“Yes.”

It was a small exchange, complete in a few words. The doorway recalled a time when neither had understood what the other was trying to protect.

### FM01 — The same doorway, years not specified [FG-F01]

**FLASHBACK — A separate earlier Blackhaven witness-protection incident.**

**Fox viewpoint:** Fox had followed a physical lead into the lane. Finding an officer between him and the next turn felt like arriving just in time to be told he was too late. He noticed the uniform before he noticed the worker waiting behind it, and later would remember that order with irritation.

**Gordon viewpoint:** Gordon had a person waiting to leave and an unfamiliar armed man approaching. He could not afford to treat confidence as authorisation. He also could not afford to ignore the way the man had stopped speaking to listen toward the bend.

**Shared scene:**

“Hold there,” Gordon said.

“I need that lane.”

“Then tell me why.”

Fox looked beyond the doorway. “Somebody stopped around the turn.”

“Somebody?”

“Footsteps. Then nothing. Close enough that I would check before taking anyone out.”

Gordon changed his angle and checked. He did not announce that the sound proved an offence. The worker behind him moved forward, then stopped when another person visibly tried to obstruct the departure.

Fox stepped toward the lane.

“Not past me,” Gordon said.

“I'm looking at the obstruction.”

“And I have somebody trying to get out.”

Fox looked behind him properly for the first time.

A brief silence. Then he moved to the street-facing position rather than farther into the lane. Gordon escorted the worker clear while the visible obstruction was dealt with in the limited authored incident. No new named arrest, killing or spectacular victory is supplied.

When the immediate danger had passed, Fox turned back.

“You could have said you were moving someone.”

“You could have asked before choosing the direction.”

“Would you have told me?”

“Enough to keep you out of the doorway.”

Fox gave a short breath that was not quite a laugh.

“You did check.”

“You gave me something worth checking.”

“That sounded painful to admit.”

“It was less painful than ignoring it.”

Neither offered friendship. They had an account to compare and a person whose safety did not become less important because they had begun arguing.

**Present return, Fox:** He took the agreed position. Gordon no longer had to explain the doorway to him, but Fox still wanted the question asked rather than the answer assumed.

**Present return, Gordon:** The brief acknowledgment from Fox let Gordon return to the shopkeeper without looking over his shoulder to guess where his partner had gone. It was cooperation built out of something they had once done badly.

### FD03 — Terms of help [FG03]

The shopkeeper pointed to a date.

“That was the second demand. I paid the first because I thought perhaps it would end.”

Fox looked up. “Did they say it would?”

“Not in those words.”

Gordon waited while the shopkeeper considered the distinction.

“I wanted it to mean that,” they said.

Fox nodded once. “All right.”

There were copies of three demands and an account of repeated pressure. No confession from an entire organisation. Gordon stated the difference in the record without making the person repeat the frightening part for emphasis.

Outside the conversation, Fox spoke more quietly.

“We know the point they use. Waiting has a cost.”

“So does acting on an incomplete route,” Gordon said.

“I didn't say wander in.”

“No. Tell me what you want to check first.”

Fox indicated the physical comparison he wanted to make. Gordon described the documentation and protected contact arrangement still needed.

**Fox viewpoint:** It helped that Gordon asked for the next action instead of asking him to become a more patient person. Fox could explain an action. He could not promise never to resent the waiting.

**Gordon viewpoint:** Fox's urgency was not an obstacle to be removed from the plan. It identified the point at which the plan might become an excuse for delay. Gordon still needed the route corroborated, and said so.

They divided the immediate work explicitly. The shopkeeper was told what they could expect next and what remained uncertain. No one promised that handing over papers would make tomorrow safe by itself.

## Chapter 2 — A trace is not a person

### FM02 — An introduction after the noise [FG-F02]

**Present return:** Fox set the current rail receipt beside the completed report. “I'll come for the next check.” Gordon nodded. Neither named the offer loyalty. Both knew it had not been automatic at the beginning.
