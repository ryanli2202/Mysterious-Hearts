## Required premise

**Shared campaign presentation requirement, 2026-11-11:** Follow [MH-016](CAMPAIGN_PRESENTATION_UPDATE_PLAN.md) for Maps-based destination travel, story-controlled location reveal/access, continuous same-building gauntlets, and per-shot visual novel images with sentence-per-line text in the in-person narration window and page-flip transitions. Story remains the objective/journal entry point; this supersedes older direct-travel or separate-reader assumptions only. Sylvanis's history, character and campaign outcome requirements are unchanged; implementation remains pending.

Sylvanis is a purely chaotic evil protagonist. He acts from impulse, hedonism, chaos and curiosity, doing things simply because he can. He has **no moral development, redemption, hidden conscience or eventual loyalty**. His circumstances and other people's responses change; his fundamental character does not. Learning a technique, discovering information or revising a tactic is not an ethical awakening.

**Agency requirement, 2026-11-11:** Sylvanis is **not manipulated in any way, shape or form**. He goes along with events because he thinks they will be fun. Make his own willing choice legible in the scene before he acts: no deception, coercion, grooming, emotional pressure, flattery, supernatural control or calculated bait causes his participation. The conspirator presents an opportunity that Sylvanis immediately chooses for his own amusement. This applies throughout recruitment, the coup, later cooperation and the present confrontation, in narration, dialogue, choices and visual staging. He remains responsible for his actions.

The following history is fixed for this campaign:

1. Humans captured his Blisk planet during the Blisk war. He moved to Earth in the middle of that war and immediately became a criminal.
2. He immediately mastered all human martial arts and established his reputation as the most feared man on the planet. Do not turn this into a long training arc or a reputation acquired only after joining the Syndicate.
3. The mercenary group called **the Organisation**, which genuinely stood up for the weak, recruited him. Its leader was **Kaden Mourne**, an older, muscular man who wore mercenary gear and was kind, with genuine goodwill. Sylvanis was content because its work gave him an excuse to fight and kill, not because he believed in its cause.
4. A member close to Kaden proposed killing him and converting the Organisation into a multinational criminal syndicate, bringing a proposed card-and-suit system. Sylvanis immediately agreed because he thought it would be fun, with no loyalty to Kaden and no manipulation or persuasion causing his decision. Before dying, Kaden asks Sylvanis whether he will ever truly find the thrill he is looking for. The betrayer/conspirator eventually became the Crow. Flashbacks must present him with a **blank face and glasses**, concealing his identity and appearance. It is **heavily implied that he is Kaden's son**, without explicitly confirming the relationship or revealing his civil identity.
5. Over time, the Crow came to tolerate Sylvanis rather than like him, because Sylvanis acts spontaneously for personal gain. Their cooperation is useful, difficult and increasingly strained.
6. Sylvanis began developing Red Magic and Blue Magic on a whim, experimented on live humans and used his own underground arena to test the drugs on strong fighters.
7. After hearing of strong fighters in the east, he sought out and killed Garrett's parents. He was disappointed that they were weak compared with him. The murders were his choice, not an accident, a defensive necessity or an order that absolves him.
8. Years later Charles joined him. Sylvanis secretly knew Charles was infiltrating and allowed it because he found it entertaining.
9. He eventually met Takeo Mishima and began a business arrangement for the Syndicate to supply Red Magic and Blue Magic to the Shinokami.
10. In the present he wants more chaos and deliberately makes the Crow's life harder to test his patience. The campaign must bring that provocation to a climax and a conclusion.

These premises supersede contradictory older planning/runtime descriptions when implemented. This document does not itself update the running game or make every character aware of these facts.

## The Organisation, Kaden Mourne and the concealed conspirator

**User-established expansion, 2026-11-11:** the Organisation is the mercenary group's actual name, and Kaden Mourne is its leader. He is an older, muscular man in mercenary gear, with sincere kindness and goodwill. The conspirator who later becomes the Crow has a blank face and glasses throughout his flashback appearances. His being Kaden's son is a strong narrative implication, not an explicit identity reveal. The scene beats below are proposed ways to express these requirements.

### Kaden and the Organisation

Use **the Organisation** consistently for the protective mercenary group before the coup, and **the Syndicate** for its criminal successor. Kaden's leadership is established; whether he founded the group, its exact age and his precise age remain unspecified.

Kaden should look experienced and physically formidable: an older face, substantial muscle, practical mercenary clothing and field equipment. His warmth comes through his attention, posture, speech and conduct. Detailed gear colours, weapons, scars and other identifying embellishments remain artwork decisions to review, rather than additional established lore.

Demonstrate his goodwill in concrete actions. In the proposed recruitment mission, he first makes sure the people being protected have shelter and medical attention, then checks his own wounded before discussing payment or Sylvanis's performance. He listens to people who cannot offer him anything in return. His protective work is sincere; do not later explain it away as a criminal front, manipulation or concealed cruelty. Kindness does not make him incompetent, and his strength does not require him to match Sylvanis.

Sylvanis recognises what Kaden values and feels no obligation to share it. Their cooperation works only because that work supplies opponents and opportunities for violence. Kaden's death remains a betrayal of a good man; it does not become a justified removal of a secretly abusive leader or a source of remorse for Sylvanis.

### Flashback appearance and identity boundaries

The conspirator's face is deliberately **blank and featureless, with glasses visibly present**. Do not draw readable eyes, brows, nose, mouth, facial contours that identify him, or a recognisable likeness behind the lenses. Use restrained, non-identifying head/body staging and generic period-appropriate clothing; hair, skin detail, distinctive markings, reflections and alternate angles must not reconstruct his concealed appearance. Glasses are the intended visible cue. A fully rendered face hidden only by lens glare or a temporary shadow does not satisfy the blank-face requirement.

This is a storytelling convention that withholds appearance from the audience, not a literal deformity, injury, mask or new supernatural ability. Other characters interact with him normally. Apply it to every flashback shot containing the conspirator, including recruitment/background appearances, the proposal, the coup, thumbnails and historical rereads. Later-era flashbacks depicting him as the Crow retain his established opaque costume and mask; any pre-mask or otherwise unmasked depiction still uses the blank face and glasses. Present-day Crow scenes retain his established concealed appearance.

Express his intent through dialogue, timing, hands and posture rather than facial expressions. Historical speaker labels use a neutral role such as **Conspirator** until the Crow title is appropriate. Narration, captions, character cards, accessible image descriptions and future asset names must not supply a civil name, a surname inferred from Kaden, an unmasked likeness or a confirmed family relationship. The reader can understand that this conspirator becomes the Crow without learning his civil identity.

### Strongly implied parentage

Build the father–son implication through repeated personal familiarity and Kaden's particular care for him, not physical resemblance. Proposed beats across the existing historical scenes:

- **SY-M03 — familiarity before the plot:** after tending to the mission's casualties, Kaden sets aside food for the bespectacled man without asking what he wants. A quiet exchange recalls the younger man's habit of waiting for Kaden to return from deployments since childhood. Other mercenaries treat this familiarity as longstanding. The scene still belongs to a working mercenary unit, with no confirmed family label.
- **SY-M04 — trust before betrayal:** Kaden invites him to stay after the formal briefing, giving him a place beside him that requires no rank or permission. Proposed line: “You don't have to earn your place beside me.” Later, the conspirator exploits Kaden's trust to arrange the coup and openly proposes it to Sylvanis. Sylvanis immediately agrees because it sounds fun; Kaden's trust is being betrayed, while Sylvanis is a willing participant whose motives are entirely his own. The conspirator's exact family history and deeper motive remain unconfirmed.
- **SY-M05 — the cost of that trust:** when the coup becomes clear, Kaden addresses the conspirator with personal concern and disbelief rather than treating him as an ordinary mutineer. A repeated gesture or phrase from the earlier scene makes the betrayal intimate without “son”, “father”, a shared surname, genealogy or a deathbed confirmation. Sylvanis remains unmoved.

The accumulation should make the son reading compelling while leaving it an inference. Do not add a public dossier, proof document, explicit narrator confirmation or present-day confession to settle it. Reader suspicion does not automatically become Sylvanis's stated knowledge or any other character's knowledge. Kaden's kindness stays genuine regardless of what the conspirator chooses to do; no invented abusive upbringing excuses the betrayal.

### Kaden's final question

**Required scene beat, 2026-11-11:** before Kaden dies in SY-M05 / SY05, he directly asks Sylvanis whether he will ever truly find the thrill he is looking for. The question belongs in the actual death scene and is addressed to Sylvanis. It must not be replaced by a journal summary, a posthumous voice-over or a question addressed only to the conspirator.

**Proposed wording:** “Sylvanis... will you ever truly find the thrill you're looking for?”

Stage enough time for Kaden to speak before his death resolves. His question comes from a kind man confronting Sylvanis's freely chosen appetite for excitement. It does not absolve Sylvanis, suggest someone led him astray, reveal the conspirator's parentage or trigger remorse. Sylvanis's exact reply or silence remains a manuscript choice; any response preserves his amusement, curiosity or indifference and complete responsibility. Do not turn the question into proof of secret suffering, a need for healing or a redemption arc.

The later hunt for stronger fighters and the present arena can give the question thematic weight through Sylvanis's continued pursuit of fun. That pursuit already motivates him before Kaden speaks; the question does not plant it, manipulate him or become a command he spends his life obeying. Whether he ever finds a lasting thrill remains open rather than introducing a new fixed answer.

## Story shape and tone

**Dramatic question:** how far will Sylvanis push the organisation he helped create before the Crow decides that tolerating him costs more than confronting him?

The opening presents a routine commercial demonstration for the Shinokami. Sylvanis finds its predictable outcome intolerably dull. Historical scenes show that each institution in his life has become a supply of new experiences: Earth, the protective mercenaries, the Syndicate, the laboratory and the arena. Present-day chapters let him deliberately spoil a profitable arrangement to produce a response he has not seen before.

The tension comes from a brilliant, immensely dangerous man deciding what would entertain him, and capable people trying to keep their own aims alive around him. Avoid a sequence of disposable victims followed by interchangeable boasts. Show the specific promise he breaks, the person who has to absorb its cost and the next problem his curiosity creates. Other characters possess agency and can achieve objectives he fails to stop.

His arrival after planetary conquest provides chronology, not a psychological excuse. No invented bereavement, persecution, righteous anti-human revenge or lost innocence explains his evil. The mercenaries' kindness does not almost redeem him. Charles does not become his surrogate son. Admiration for an opponent does not become honour. He may be funny, perceptive or briefly courteous without being safe or sympathetic.

Use close third-person prose, authored dialogue and playable action through the existing campaign systems. His internal narration can reveal that he knows Charles's cover is false while his spoken dialogue maintains the deception. Unreliable statements belong to his voice; narration must not falsely establish an act of mercy as moral growth.

## Present-day conflict: everyone wants something different

**Sylvanis** wants a more interesting fight and to make the Crow abandon distant management for a personal response. Selling the drugs, frightening buyers and destroying his own profitable work are equally available pleasures. His spontaneous decisions should change events, not merely appear in dialogue while he obediently completes every task.

**The Crow** wants the Shinokami arrangement and his authority intact. He understands Sylvanis's danger because he helped bring him into power. He uses controlled access, personnel, records, alternative supply arrangements and explicit decisions. He can lose an exchange and still secure an organisational objective. His patience is strategic tolerance, not fondness or a moral desire to save Sylvanis.

His strategic competence never depends on manipulating Sylvanis. Show direct orders, declared opposition and changes to resources rather than a revelation that he secretly engineered Sylvanis's desires or choices. Charles can also act independently and secure evidence without controlling Sylvanis's participation. Sylvanis can lack information, lose assets or fail an objective; those limits do not make him anyone's unwitting instrument, and his voluntary cooperation does not grant him omniscience or guaranteed victory.

**Charles** wants useful evidence and opportunities against the man responsible for his family tragedy. His undercover competence matters even though Sylvanis knows he is infiltrating. He can hide a particular move or preserve something Sylvanis cannot retrieve; he does not suddenly become foolish when the audience learns the secret. Keep his present scenes inside the Glasshouse. His earlier intervention can affect the arena without moving him there.

**Takeo** wants a dependable source of usable military advantage. The historical deal and present delegation give him concrete stakes. He recognises breach and responds with suspended orders, security demands or a changed liaison arrangement. He remains at his headquarters, and the campaign does not decide his defeat, death or knowledge of the Crow's planned betrayal.

**Other fighters and staff** want to survive, win, escape, get paid or perform the job they actually accepted. They are not one mind. Some may be coerced and others willing competitors; neither status makes them disposable to the narration. Sylvanis need not care for the story to register what he does to them.

## Climax: the private arena closes

The proposed climax is a deliberate breach followed by a personal confrontation, not the accidental discovery that Sylvanis went too far. He knows exactly which order he is violating and enjoys the prospect of the response.

1. **The spectacle begins.** The Shinokami delegation expects an agreed product demonstration. Sylvanis intervenes personally and converts it into a dangerous exhibition against strong fighters. Drug effects follow established temporary benefits and harmful limits; no spontaneous new power makes someone his equal. Fight quality can come from multiple objectives and the circumstances, not an unexplained stronger stranger.
