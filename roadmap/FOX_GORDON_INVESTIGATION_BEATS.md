## FL01 — The cleaner on the envelope (chapter 2 / FG04)

**Why it convinces:** Fox detects the same sharp chemical note on a demand envelope and near a service entrance. His observation is accurate; the initial suggestion is that the person preparing the demands uses that entrance. A familiar smell feels more concrete than a frightened witness's uncertain description.

**Investigation:** With the shopkeeper's permission, examine the envelope and compare the entrance to other accessible nearby surfaces. Gordon asks about ordinary cleaning deliveries. A consenting worker identifies the cleaning product used by several premises; a labelled container and routine supply record corroborate this limited explanation. Fox compares the broad background scent with the more specific fibres and physically observed collection route already required by the case.

**Argument:** Fox believes waiting may let the collector slip away; Gordon worries about treating every cleaner or delivery worker as an associate. Gordon does not dismiss the sense itself.

> Fox: “The trace is there.”
>
> Gordon: “I believe you. How many other doors could it put us at?”

**Truth:** The smell is too common to uniquely identify the source. It neither clears nor implicates everyone who uses the product. The matching fibres plus observed route support the actual collection-point finding. The player can compare the checks in either order; both their limitations and the valid route must remain readable.

**Payoff:** Fox qualifies the claim himself. Gordon credits the observation in the report rather than presenting correction as a victory over him. The existing collector encounter follows the corroborated route; no fight with an innocent worker or new Syndicate patrol is added here. Conclude with the protected shopkeeper, not an abstract proof lesson.

## FL02 — The number used twice (chapter 3 / FG07–FG09)

**Why it convinces:** A public freight entry carries the recovered consignment number. Gordon initially favours it because its printed form looks complete. The number is reused in another dated ledger period; it is not a fabricated document.

**Investigation:** Read the full dated page, not a cropped number. At the depot frontage, Fox notices that the visible seal or handling mark differs from the receipt. Ask a willing worker about the ordinary numbering cycle; compare that explanation with original ledger headings and the actual dated handover. No break-in or accusation is needed to check public records and volunteered process information.

**Argument:** Gordon wants to preserve a coherent documentary trail; Fox thinks he is giving the printed page more weight than a physical mismatch. Fox must explain what he sees instead of merely asserting instinct.

> Fox: “Right number. Wrong seal.”
>
> Gordon: “Then we check which part of the match we assumed.”

**Truth:** The apparent match belongs to another period. Date, seal and provenance identify the correct handover already required by the rail case. The unrelated shipment is excluded from this enquiry before any interception; do not label its crew guilty or universally innocent beyond that finding.

**Payoff:** Gordon explicitly corrects his first reading; Fox sees the report change rather than a defensive excuse. The actual armed freight courier encounter remains against the supported target and existing faction deployment. No reward or custody is earned from the rejected shipment. At the later maintenance scene, a dry callback can acknowledge that either man's method needs the other's questions.

## FL03 — The date the contractor cannot supply (chapter 4 / FG10–FG12)

**Why it convinces:** A contractor gives a hesitant chronology while supplying genuine referral documents. Fox suspects an omission may conceal a larger role; Gordon recognises that precision cannot be demanded into existence. Neither knows why the gap exists.

**Investigation:** Ask which portions the contractor personally observed and which dates come from documents. Permit a pause. Read the original referral and independently dated delivery manifest; compare exterior observations as in the existing case. The proposed discrepancy is a document-processing date being mistaken for the date of the contractor's physical visit. Establish this through the document's actual fields and corroboration, not by diagnosing stress or reading a heartbeat.

**Argument:** Fox wants to ask the uncomfortable question before committing others to protect an unreliable account. Gordon insists that immediate protection is not conditional on perfect recollection. He still tests the account rather than declaring it beyond scrutiny. The pursuit/protection flashback FG-F03 supplies emotional context without answering the evidence question.

**Truth:** The compared dates serve different purposes; the corroborated portion of the account stands. Any remaining gap stays unresolved. The enforcer's attack demonstrates a current threat, not automatic truth of every statement or proof that the witness could never have been involved elsewhere.

**Payoff:** Fox asks a narrower follow-up and accepts what the contractor cannot answer. Gordon acknowledges that testing the dates was useful. Preserve the Shinokami enforcer encounter and its Yokai/Oni/Tengu deployment; it must not become a Syndicate guard fight merely because the next case involves the Syndicate. The recovered transfer record opens four coded desks, not four known identities.

## FL04 — The driver everyone saw (Clubs / FG13–FG15)

**Why it convinces:** A witness remembers a substitute driver taking an escort packet and giving a loading instruction. The initials on a working copy appear consistent with the driver. The tempting leap is that the driver authored the security order.

**Investigation:** Obtain the archived original first. Ask the driver, if willing, which instructions they received and what they actually decided. Compare the signed authorisation with the receiving acknowledgement and dispatch duty record. An independently witnessed deployment supplies the existing link to the operative. The driver can know their route without knowing Suit command or the identity behind a mask.

**Argument:** Fox wants to keep watching a possible link rather than close it prematurely. Gordon objects to moving from handing over paper to issuing the order. Fox points out that the driver may still be useful as a witness; Gordon agrees without turning the interview into detention.

> Fox: “He carried the instruction.”
>
> Gordon: “Then ask what he carried. Not why he wrote it.”

**Truth:** The receiving initials acknowledge delivery; the original authorisation and independently witnessed operation establish the actual command link. The driver's visible role does not prove command responsibility. Record the narrow conclusion, not a sweeping clearance certificate.

**Guard sequence:** After the actual case reveal, use **FG-C01** below before the masked specialist confrontation. Do not postpone or award the existing pre-combat reveal through a guard's confession. Guards oppose the authorised field team because of the supported operation, not because the player accused a civilian.

**Payoff:** Gordon gives Fox room to follow the useful part of his suspicion; Fox separates being connected to an event from directing it. The driver can decline further participation. After victory, review the guard/Ace outcomes and command material without implying the entire Suit is dismantled.

**Argument:** Gordon initially finds the coherent public account worth checking; Fox distrusts how neatly a crowd's story fills gaps. Fox can be right about the mismatch without asserting that everyone is lying. Gordon asks for a test that could actually distinguish the accounts.

**Truth:** The rumour's timeline cannot place that performer at the documented masked deployment. The actual original instruction and independent observation support the existing Elias link. **Guard sequence:** **FG-C02** handles the supported confrontation route after the actual reveal. The player never attacks the misidentified performer or punishes the witness for an honest error.

**Payoff:** A brief private correction to the people actually interviewed prevents the investigators themselves repeating the allegation; do not invent a public broadcast, media scandal or automatic reputational damage. The later argument asks how readily both men accepted a satisfying story, rather than making Gordon deliver another lecture to Fox.
