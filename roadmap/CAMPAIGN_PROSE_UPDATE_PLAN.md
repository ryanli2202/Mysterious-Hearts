### Six prose layers

1. Campaign openings establish the protagonist's situation, personal stakes and reason to act.
2. Scene arrivals establish atmosphere through what the viewpoint character notices and cares about.
3. Prose between dialogue lines conveys body language, hesitation, tension and relevant inner thoughts; avoid explaining every line or repeating what dialogue already communicates.
4. Discovery passages distinguish what the protagonist understands, suspects and cannot yet answer, and why the discovery matters personally.
5. Objective transitions explain why the next destination follows from the evidence and motivation, including uncertainty and emotional cost.
6. Aftermath passages give grief, relief, conflict and relationship changes room to register before further objectives.

### Agreed supporting features

- Establish each campaign's immediate situation, personal stakes and reason to act in an opening scene.
- Build important scenes around arrival and atmosphere, character intention, action/dialogue, interpretation and a motivated next step. Scale length to dramatic importance rather than padding every interaction.
- Explain why a clue matters using only what the viewpoint character currently knows. Distinguish facts, suspicions and unresolved questions.
- Connect locations through causal transitions: why this destination, why now, and what the character expects or fears there.
- Give flashbacks a present-day trigger, a clear time/place transition and a return to the present that shows their emotional or practical significance. Do not reveal future secrets.
- Add aftermath scenes following discoveries, conflict and chapter endings so consequences register before the next objective.
- Offer a saved, spoiler-safe story-so-far recap and readable scene history. Core context belongs in scenes rather than requiring journal reading.
- Prefer authored prose for plot-critical facts, motives and revelations. Optional generated connective description must remain bounded by authored context and resolved outcomes, with an authored fallback.
- Prototype one complete chapter before expanding across campaigns. Novel-like narration, inner thoughts and close third-person viewpoint are confirmed; decide desired reading density and pilot campaign with the user.

Short transitions may need only a few sentences; major confrontations can support longer passages interspersed with dialogue and meaningful choices. A flashback must return to the present with emotional or practical significance, rather than acting as an isolated interruption. The illustrative Garrett station passage discussed in chat was an example of style, not new canonical lore.

## Additional confirmed scope — 2026-11-10

- **Close third-person viewpoint:** Stay with one character's perceptions and inner voice per scene. Other characters' thoughts remain unknown unless revealed through established means. Use explicit breaks for any viewpoint change.
- **Distinct narrative voices:** Develop a short voice guide for each protagonist covering attention, vocabulary, emotional defences and thought patterns. Avoid giving everyone the same reflective narrator.
- **Emotional continuity:** Track relevant authored scene outcomes so later prose acknowledges earlier arguments, promises, discoveries and unresolved feelings. Use explicit narrative flags rather than inferring private feelings from a generic relationship score.
- **Reflection choices:** At selected dramatic moments, let the player choose what to ask, admit, conceal or focus on. Follow choices with appropriate prose; do not offer false branching or silently change established personality. For custom protagonists, player-selected attitudes can guide interiority.
- **Quiet scenes and recurring details:** Include occasional low-pressure moments and recurring objects, places or phrases whose meaning develops with the story. Tie these to existing lore rather than creating arbitrary backstory.
- **Reading comfort:** Offer adjustable text size, immediate text display, saved reading position and scene history. If skipping is supported, preserve required choices and show essential context without silently choosing an answer.
- **Chapter-level editorial review:** Review motivation, emotional change, pacing, repetition and spoiler boundaries across whole chapters, including a cold read by someone without external lore knowledge. Prose length alone is not a quality measure.
