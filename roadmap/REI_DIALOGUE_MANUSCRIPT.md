## Chapter 1 — Two pages on the table

### RD01 — The blank spaces [R1; existing opening]

Rei knew the notebook belonged to him before he recognised anything inside it.

There was a pressure to the handwriting, the same habit of bearing down at the end of a line. He still did it when he was tired. He passed a finger beside the letters without touching them.

A timetable. Instructions divided into intervals.

He remembered counting those intervals. That much came without effort. When he tried to follow the memory farther, there was no room around it, no continuous sequence he could walk through. He looked up at his own table and waited until the present settled around him again.

Yuriko had left the book with him years ago. It had not arrived today with a message he had failed to understand. Nothing about the paper had changed because he had finally opened it.

He brought out a drawing made after they had escaped and placed it beside the timetable.

The difference between the pages bothered him. He had expected to be able to recognise himself more easily in one than the other. Instead he recognised the pressure of his hand in both.

“I made this too,” he said.

The words sounded oddly formal in the empty room. He looked at the drawing again.

No one had assigned it to him. He had chosen where the first line went.

**Existing choice: record the fragment.** He wrote down what he could actually read and what he remembered of counting. When he reached the point where the memory stopped, he stopped writing. The empty space looked deliberate now. He left it there.

**Existing choice: pause and ground himself.** He closed the book halfway, stood and felt the floor beneath both feet. The table was his table. The drawing was still beside the notebook when he sat again. He recorded only what was on the page.

The old seal matched the name on a present-day Kurose House notice. A name surviving did not tell him who had survived with it, or whether anyone had. He wanted records he could check without asking his memory to manufacture the missing years.

### RD02 — You can close the book [RS01; later conversation with Yuriko]

Yuriko stopped when she saw the notebook.

“You found it.”

“I knew where it was.” Rei looked down. “I opened it.”

She nodded, accepting the difference. For a moment he wished she would say something ordinary enough to make him stop waiting for her reaction.

“Do you want me to sit?” she asked.

He moved a chair away from the table with his foot.

**If he shares the page:**

“I recognised the writing,” he said. “Then I couldn't tell whether I remembered what it said or just remembered being told things like it.”

Yuriko leaned forward only far enough to see the part he turned toward her.

“I don't want to answer that for you.”

“You were there.”

“Not everywhere you were.”

He looked at her. She was holding herself carefully, as though trying not to take up space he had not offered.

“I know,” he said. “Sorry.”

“You can ask me. I just need to be allowed to say I don't know.”

Rei glanced at the other drawing. “I keep thinking I should be able to look at a page.”

“You are looking at it.”

“Without doing this.”

“Doing what?”

He couldn't find a description that didn't sound smaller than it felt.

Yuriko waited. Then she said, “Would you like me to put the other things away? Or leave them?”

“Leave them.”

“All right.”

He let the notebook close. She did not look relieved, which he was grateful for. It would have made him feel as though he had completed a task for her.

**If he keeps it private:**

“I don't want to show you yet.”

“Then don't.” She pulled the chair a little farther from the page. “Do you want me here anyway?”

He nodded.

“Can we talk about something else?”

“Yes. But you might have to help me choose it.”

That made him look up. She gave him a small, uncertain smile. He had been so occupied with finding the right response that he had forgotten she might be searching too.

### RD03 — The usual [GR-R01]

Garrett looked up when Rei came in.

“The usual?”

Rei took longer than he meant to answer.

“Or something else,” Garrett added. “I do know more than one order.”

“The usual is fine.”

“Good. I'd have had to defend my reputation.”

Rei sat where he could watch Garrett work. The familiarity of it loosened something he had been carrying without noticing: a question asked because someone wanted to feed him, an answer that did not need supporting documents.

When Garrett set the plate down, one dumpling had opened along its seam.

“Before you say anything—”

“I wasn't going to.”

“You're looking at it professionally.”

Rei turned the plate a little. “It has character.”

Garrett stopped, then laughed. “Still using that excuse?”

The old phrase brought back the first visit so clearly that for a moment the room seemed to contain both evenings.

### RD04 — The split dumpling [GR-F01; historical first meeting]

**FLASHBACK — Golden Dragon, early months; after Rei began modelling.**

Rei had come in alone after a shoot. He wanted food and a place where no one was asking him to hold a position. His sketchbook rested against his leg beneath the table.

Garrett noticed the split dumpling as soon as he put the plate down.

“Give me that one. I'll replace it.”

“It's all right.”

“It's opened.”

“I can see that.”

Garrett looked at him, trying to decide whether he was being difficult. Rei hadn't meant to be. He was simply tired of moving something because it didn't look right.

He pulled the receipt closer and drew the crooked seam.

When Garrett returned, he glanced at the paper.

“Is that a complaint?”

Rei looked from the drawing to the plate. “It has more character than the intact ones.”

“That sounds suspiciously like a polite complaint.”

“I ate it.”

“A committed critic.”

Rei felt himself smile before he decided whether to encourage him.

Garrett pointed at the receipt. “Can I keep that?”

“What for?”

“To remember the standard I'm being held to.”

“It isn't a standard.”

“Good. I was worried I'd have to break one on every plate.”

Rei pushed the paper toward him. Garrett pinned it up rather than dropping it beside the till. Rei watched the small care with which he straightened it.

“You don't have to display it.”

“I know.”

For once, Rei had no answer ready. He finished his meal more slowly than he needed to.

**PRESENT RETURN:** Garrett was still standing beside the table, watching Rei's expression.

“You went quiet.”

“I was remembering.”

“The review?”

“The first time.”

Garrett's smile softened. “You can stay a while this time too.”

Rei looked at the food. He had records to ask for, and he would ask for them. He did not have to leave before finishing dinner to prove how much they mattered.

## Chapter 2 — What the paper remembers

### RD05 — A name that survived [R2; archive and private aftermath]

The dates were clear.

Rei checked them again anyway, following the same line with his finger. The original organisation had ended. A later one had taken the name. Records had passed between hands; that did not mean the people he remembered had returned.

He had been afraid of an answer he could recognise immediately. The actual one required him to sit still and read.

The retained training ledger described imposed drills and punishment. His name occupied a space in a record someone else had decided to keep. There was no account of what the room had felt like to him, no indication that anyone had considered the intervals too long for a child.

He took the copy he was permitted to make and put it beside his notes.

“So it happened.”

He regretted saying it. He had not needed the ledger to make his life real. Yet seeing the corroboration affected him, and he did not know how to admit that without sounding as though he had doubted himself completely.

The filing did not restore a voice, a face or a missing hour. He waited for something to arrive. Nothing did.

After a while he wrote: *The document confirms this. I do not remember all of it.*

Both sentences could stay.

The current records named Kurose House, the riverside annex and the supply house, along with the successor's administrators. Those were places and people he could ask about in the present. He copied their dates carefully, keeping the old organisation and the new one on separate lines.

**Choice handling:** Copy the records or annotate their gaps according to the existing selected response. Preserve identical corroborated findings. This scene supplies no recovered memory, biological-parent information or new escape detail.

### RD06 — The version I remember [RS02; conditional disclosure to Yuriko]

“I found records,” Rei said.

Yuriko looked at the folder but did not reach for it. “Do you want me to read them?”

“Not all of them.”

He selected the page he was prepared to share. It took longer than handing her the whole folder would have. She waited without watching his hands too closely.

When she finished, she put it down between them.

“I don't remember this happening to you.”

“I didn't think you would.”

“I don't mean—”

“I know.”

But he had answered too quickly. Her face changed, and he realised she had been trying to say more.

“Sorry,” he said. “Go on.”

“I mean I can believe you without having a memory to put beside it.”

He looked at the page. “I keep asking myself why I remember one part so clearly and not the thing next to it.”

“I can't tell you why.” She folded her hands in her lap. “There are things I remember differently from the way you talk about them. I worry you'll think I'm saying yours are wrong.”

“I don't want you to agree with me just to make it easier.”

“Then don't ask yourself to agree with a whole record at once either. Read what it actually says.”

He almost smiled. “You sound like you've been waiting to tell me that.”

“I've been waiting for a chance to finish a sentence.”

He gave a brief, surprised laugh. She looked relieved for herself this time, and he understood it.

“Finish it.”

“I want us to be able to talk without one of us having to remember enough for both.”

Rei moved the folder away from the space between them.

“I want that too.”

### RD07 — Something still being made [RS03; Emily, accessible creative visit]

Emily had two pieces of paper laid out beside her work.

“Which one?” she asked.

Rei looked at them. “For what?”

“The backing. The first one makes the colour softer. The second makes it stand out.”

“What do you want it to do?”

She gave him a look. “I was hoping you would answer that part.”

He bent closer. For several minutes he had to think about nothing but the edges of the paper and the way one colour changed another. He hadn't noticed how hungry he was for a question of that size.

“The second,” he said. “But turn it.”

“Like this?”

“A little more.”

She moved it too far on purpose.

“Perfect,” he said. “Now no one can see the work at all.”

Emily laughed and set it straight.

After a while she asked, “Do you want to tell me how you've been?”

He kept looking at the paper. “I don't know how to make it a short answer.”

“It doesn't have to be short.”

“I don't know if I want the long one either.”

She nodded. “Then you can hold this corner while I fix it.”

“You don't mind?”

“I asked you here because I wanted your opinion. I still want it.”

He placed his hand where she indicated. She adjusted the backing, frowned, then reached for the first sheet again.

“You're ignoring my opinion.”

“I'm considering it. That's different.”

He smiled. For once, being unable to settle something did not mean either of them was in trouble.

### RD08 — No need to fill the silence [GR-R02 and GR-F02]

**Present, at a later Golden Dragon visit; Garrett knows Rei has memory gaps, not the new private findings unless shared.**

“Do you want an answer,” Garrett asked, “or do you want me to listen?”

“I haven't asked anything yet.”

“You've been staring at the same clean bowl for a while.”

Rei set it down. “What if I tell you something and then have to say I don't remember the part you're going to ask about?”

“Then I'll stop asking about that part.”

“You make it sound easy.”

“I didn't say I'd never get it wrong.” Garrett passed him a dry cloth. “You can tell me when I do.”

Rei took it. The small division of work—the wet bowls, the drying, the places they knew without asking—brought back how long it had taken to become ordinary.

**FLASHBACK — Later visits, after their first meeting.**

Garrett remembered his order the next time. Rei told himself that was a good reason to return again. The food was good. It was on his way often enough. He could sit without being hurried.

On a later evening, Garrett asked about the sketchbook and actually waited while Rei explained what he was trying to make.

Another night, Rei stayed while the bowls were washed.

“You don't have to do that,” Garrett said when Rei picked up a cloth.

“I know.”

Garrett looked at him, then slid a bowl within reach.

The silence after that lasted long enough for Rei to wonder whether he was expected to repair it. Before he could think of a subject, Garrett began talking about something that had gone wrong in the kitchen. He didn't ask why Rei was quiet.

Eventually there was an evening when Rei came in after he had already eaten.

Garrett glanced toward the kitchen. “Food?”

“No. I just—”

He nearly supplied an excuse. Garrett pulled out a chair.

“All right. Sit down.”

**PRESENT RETURN:** Rei finished drying the bowl.

“I don't have a whole story to tell you tonight.”

Garrett placed another bowl beside it. “You didn't have one the first time either.”

“I had a complaint.”

“No, apparently you had an artistic assessment.”

Rei shook his head. He stayed. He could share a little, or nothing more, and the chair remained his to use.

## Chapter 3 — Marcus, beyond what happened

### RD09 — The shot they kept [R3; expands rei-school-friends]

**Present trigger:** Rei opens the kept storyboard alone. Its crooked miniature roof recalls the work of holding it still, before the page recalls any later loss.

**FLASHBACK — High-school lunch break.**

“Hold the pigeon there,” Marcus said. “No, higher. It has a very important bus to miss.”

Rei looked at the timetable. “This says it arrives yesterday.”

“Public transport with ambition.”

“That's not what ambition means.”

“Fine. Public transport with a serious administrative problem. Hold still.”

The roof slipped. Rei caught it before it flattened the cardboard street.

Marcus lowered his hands. “A very local weather event.”

“You built a roof that doesn't fit.”

“You moved the building.”

“Because the pigeon couldn't get past it.”

For a moment neither spoke. Marcus looked between the pieces, annoyed enough that Rei wondered whether he should offer to start again.

Instead Marcus picked up the pencil.

“If I make the street wider, it stops looking like the place I wanted.”

“Then let it get in the way.”

“What?”

“The pigeon can walk around it.” Rei shifted the little bird along the edge of the building. “It misses the bus. It goes past the places the bus doesn't.”

Marcus watched him move it. Then he leaned over the page.

“Wait. Do that again.”

Rei did.

“Slower.”

“You could say please.”

“Please perform the extremely important pigeon journey again.”

Rei laughed. Marcus was already drawing, tongue briefly caught between his teeth as he concentrated.

“You're changing my whole bit,” Rei said.

“I'm fixing the part where you made it interesting and didn't tell me where it ended.”

“So it was interesting.”

“Don't make me regret saying it.”

They tried the movement again. It took longer than either of them expected. Marcus changed his mind twice and refused to change it a third time. Rei complained, then held the roof where it needed to be.

“You're buying lunch after this.”

“Half my sandwich and a credit bigger than the pigeon.”

“Deal.”

**PRESENT RETURN:** Adult Rei rested his hand beside the drawing. He remembered being hungry and irritated and pleased with their idea. He let all three things remain in the memory. Marcus had been there making decisions, not waiting to become the subject of somebody else's grief.

### RD10 — More than the worst day [RS05; Emily and Sophie]

Emily looked at the storyboard Rei had chosen to bring.

“He could be so stubborn about it.”

Rei glanced up.

She hesitated. “I don't mean that badly.”

“No,” he said. “He could.”

Sophie leaned forward without taking the page. “You were both very certain a cardboard bird needed that much discussion.”

“It did.”

“Apparently.”

Rei found himself defending the idea before he remembered he didn't have to. “It wasn't just the bird. He wanted it to feel like a place.”

Emily nodded. “I liked listening to him explain things. Even when I didn't know how he got from the first thing to the last.”

“He'd get there,” Rei said.

“Eventually,” Sophie said.

He laughed. The laugh stopped abruptly, leaving him embarrassed by how exposed he felt.

Emily looked down at her hands. “I do that too.”

“What?”

“Laugh. Then feel as though I've forgotten something.”

Rei ran his thumb along the edge of the paper. “Sometimes I feel like I'm being dishonest if I talk about a good day.”

Sophie waited before answering. “I don't think a good day stops having happened.”

“I know. It just feels strange.”

“Yes,” Emily said. “It does.”

Neither rushed to make it feel different. Rei looked at the drawing again and told them how Marcus had refused to widen the street. The disagreement mattered to the story. He left it in.

### RD12 — A walk without a destination [RS04; Sophie]

“Do you have somewhere in mind?” Sophie asked.

“No.”

“Good. I was going to pretend I did.”

Rei looked at her. She lifted one shoulder, and he felt the first small release of tension since leaving home.

They walked for a while without discussing why he had asked to meet. He noticed that she slowed when he did without making an announcement of it.

“You can walk normally,” he said.

“I am.”

“You're matching me.”

“That is normal when I'm walking with someone.”

He almost apologised. Instead he said, “I don't know what I want to talk about.”

“Then we can find out.”

“And if I don't?”

“We'll still have walked somewhere.”

A few steps later he said, “I remembered the bench.”

Sophie looked ahead. “Oh.”

He appreciated the smallness of the answer. She didn't ask him to describe everything.

“I thought I was remembering the film. Then I was back there.”

“Do you want to stop?”

“No. Keep walking.”

She did.

After a while she said, “I don't always know whether asking makes it worse.”

“It helps that you ask me.”

“All right.”

“And that you don't look disappointed when I don't know.”

Sophie gave him a brief sideways glance. “I have plenty of other things to be disappointed in. Your directions, for example.”

“We don't have a destination.”

“Exactly. Terrible planning.”

He laughed softly. They kept walking, and he did not have to explain the laugh either.

### RD13 — What anger could not repair [rei-school-suspension]

**Present trigger:** Alone again, Rei remembers Joseph treating the bullying as something too small to answer for. This historical scene follows the news and a present-day interval; it is not a present-day revenge choice.

**FLASHBACK — Outside the school administration entrance.**

“Joseph.”

Joseph turned, impatience already on his face. “What?”

Rei had wanted him to turn. Now that he had, the words Rei had rehearsed no longer came in order.

“You don't get to talk about him like that.”

“Like what?”

“Like none of it mattered.”

Joseph looked toward the building. “I'm not doing this.”

“You took his things. You made everybody look. You waited until people were watching.”

“It was school. People get picked on.”

“His name was Marcus.”

“I know his name.”

“Then say it.”

Joseph tried to move past him. Rei stepped into his way.

“You think shouting at me changes anything?” Joseph said.

Rei wanted a reaction that would make the weeks of cruelty visible. He wanted Joseph to stop looking inconvenienced.

He shoved him against the wall and punched him. Joseph staggered and raised his arms. Rei lunged again before a teacher pulled him back.

“Rei. Step back. Now.”

“Let go.”

Another member of staff moved toward Joseph. Rei struggled once, then stopped when he realised he was still trying to reach him.

“You saw what he did,” he said. He was no longer sure who he was saying it to. “Where were you then?”

“We will take a statement about that. Right now, you attacked another student. You're coming to the office.”

Later, the suspension decision lay between him and the headteacher. His guardian would be contacted. There would be a return meeting. The bullying report would be addressed separately.

“So he gets to go back,” Rei said, “and act like it was nothing.”

“The suspension is for your actions today.”

He looked at his hand. It hurt. He had imagined Joseph's pain would make him understand something. He could not tell whether Joseph understood anything more than he had before.

Outside, Rei folded the letter around the unfinished storyboard. He walked away with both of them.

**PRESENT RETURN:** He laid the remembered sequence beside the present feeling without pretending he was still in it. Being angry had not been the same as knowing what to do. He needed to remember that before deciding what came next.

### RD14 — The story you told me [GR-R03; Garrett]

Garrett put the clean towel down when Rei mentioned Marcus.

“The film?” he asked.

Rei nodded. “I keep coming back to it.”

“You told me about the bus timetable.”

“And the pigeon.”

“And the weather event.” Garrett's expression shifted into a small smile. “You sounded annoyed with him when you told me that part.”

“Good.”

Rei looked at him.

“I mean—” Garrett stopped. “That came out wrong.”

“No. I think I know.”

Garrett leaned back against the counter. “He gets to be somebody who annoyed you too. From the way you talk about him, I don't think he'd have made that difficult.”

Rei laughed, then looked down.

“I worry I'm telling the same story too often.”

“You haven't made me learn it for a test.”

“Garrett.”

“I don't mind hearing it.” The answer came without a joke this time. “I didn't know him. This is how I get to know something about him.”

Rei let that sit between them.

“Joseph still makes it small,” he said eventually.

Garrett's hands tightened briefly around the edge of the counter. “I remember you telling me.”

“I don't need you to do anything.”

“All right.”

The quick agreement surprised him.

“You can be angry,” Rei said.

“I am. I can be angry and stay here.”

Rei looked toward the unfinished cleaning. “You were working.”

“It'll wait.”

For once, Rei believed him without trying to calculate how much of a burden the waiting made him.

### RD15 — Where the storyboard goes [existing Marcus-stage choice]

Back home, Rei opened to the miniature city again.

There was a temptation to make the page carry a conclusion: something he had learned, something he could finally promise. He could not find a sentence that did not ask too much of a drawing two boys had made during lunch.

**Write about Marcus:** He began with the crooked roof. Then the argument about the street. He wrote that Marcus had made him laugh and sometimes made him impatient. When he reached Joseph's bullying, he recorded what he knew without making it the only thing on the page. He did not invent the words Marcus had never had a chance to say to him.

**Set the storyboard somewhere accessible:** He chose a place he could reach without having to unpack a whole history first. He moved it twice before leaving it there. It did not have to become a display he explained to visitors. He could come back to it privately.

The gaps in his childhood memory and the grief he remembered from school were different things. He did not need to make either explain the other. He closed his notes when he had written what he could honestly say.

## Chapter 4 — What is happening now

### RD16 — The consent that was not there [Blackstone reception]

Rei nearly began with Joseph's name.

He stopped himself. The person at the desk had not been in the school courtyard. He had come for a form, and he needed to ask for the form.

“I'm looking for the public participation information.”

Robin provided the published material. Rei read the withdrawal clause once, then again.

“This refers to a separate contract.”

“The procurement reference is there.”

“Can I get a copy of that here?”

“The reference identifies the archive copy.”

He looked back at the page. “This doesn't tell me what happens if someone changes their mind.”

“I can give you the public form. I can't answer for records I don't have.”

The restraint in the answer irritated him before he recognised that it was reasonable. He had no basis to decide what Robin knew about the programme beyond the material being supplied.

“Right,” he said. “Thank you.”

He nearly added another question that was really an accusation. Instead he compared the project number and noted the source.

Outside, he stood with the form held flat between his hands. The referral and the contract shared a reference. That warranted another question. It did not turn the person at the desk into an accomplice or put him any closer to knowing what Joseph would admit.

**Existing choice:** Compare the clause/reference or photograph the public document and retain its provenance. No private files, confession or extra access is granted.

### RD17 — The experimental programme [archive; private narration]

The complaint was anonymised. Rei read it without trying to supply a face.

People had attempted to withdraw. The records described restraint trials. The authorisation bore Joseph's name.

He went back to the dates, the project number, the connection to the contract. He needed the connection to hold without his anger holding it together.

It did.

For a while he could hear nothing in the room except paper moving beneath his hands. He thought of the participation form, its careful headings, the space where a person could have believed they were being asked.

“They said they wanted to stop,” he said quietly.

This time the sentence belonged to the documents in front of him. He did not need a school memory to make the conduct wrong.

He preserved the authorisation with the complaint, or kept his unanswered questions separately, according to the choice he had made. The equipment records identified the relevant compound and offices. He copied what he would need before approaching headquarters.

He wanted to ask Joseph whether he remembered every person he had made feel small. But that was not the question that would stop the programme.

Rei closed the folder with both hands.

First, what was happening now. He could carry the rest without pretending it was the only reason to act.

### RD18 — A first draft, not a rescue [RS06; Yuriko]

“Can you read this?” Yuriko asked.

Rei reached for the pages. “Of course.”

“Actually read it.”

He looked up.

“Not the way you read something when you've already decided to tell me it's good.”

“I don't do that.”

“You get very quick at the end.”

He looked down at the draft, caught. “All right. I'll be slower.”

“That isn't exactly what I'm asking.”

He smiled and began again.

For a while she found small things to do elsewhere in the room. He could tell she was listening for the page turns. When he finished, she returned too casually.

“Well?”

“I understood what you were trying to do here.” He indicated a passage. “Then I wasn't sure why it changed.”

She frowned. “That was the part I liked.”

“You asked.”

“I know. I'm being gracious.”

“Very.”

She sat beside him and explained. He listened, then read the passage again.

“That isn't all on the page yet,” he said.

Yuriko looked annoyed for a moment, then thoughtful. “Will you read it when I change it?”

“Yes.”

“You don't have to promise immediately.”

“I want to.”

She gathered the pages, leaving the difficult one on top.

“Thank you.”

Rei had come into the room expecting to be asked how he was managing. It was a relief to have helped with something that belonged to her, and to know that helping had included disagreeing.

## Chapter 5 — A name is not an answer

### RD19 — The successor answers [Emi, Ren and Daichi; existing sixth stage]

Rei heard the name of the organisation before he sat down.

The familiarity of it did not make the room familiar. He made himself look at what was actually there: three people waiting for his questions, records placed where he could read them, a chair he had not been ordered to occupy.

Emi spoke first.

“The name frightens you for a reason.”

He stayed standing a moment longer. “You don't know all the reasons.”

“No,” she said. “I don't.”

The admission was preferable to sympathy that claimed too much.

Ren moved the records toward him. “The current roster. The departure records. You can compare them with the filings.”
