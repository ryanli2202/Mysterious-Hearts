### FG-F01 — The same doorway: first impressions under pressure

**Situation:** Before the current campaign, a Blackhaven repair-shop worker has agreed to give an account concerning a local intimidation incident. Gordon is personally arranging the worker's departure through a rear entrance. Fox has independently followed a physical trace linked to the incident to the same service lane. This is a separate historical incident, not the present-day shopkeeper case; it establishes no connection to an Ace or the Crow.

**Opening:** Fox finds Gordon in the doorway and assumes he is being kept away while an official process loses its chance to act. Gordon sees an armed, impatient stranger approaching a sensitive departure and cannot yet tell whether Fox is helping or exposing the worker. Neither begins with privileged knowledge of the other's intentions.

Fox hears someone pause beyond the bend in the lane. He reports the footfall and movement; he cannot identify guilt or intention through a heartbeat. Gordon does not dismiss the warning or accept it as proof. He changes the departure angle and checks the lane himself. When an approaching person visibly tries to obstruct the worker's exit, both respond to that observed conduct.

Their first argument is about who controls the doorway. Fox wants to move toward the obstruction; Gordon wants the worker out of it. Gordon's short explanation changes the problem: the worker is already behind him, waiting for an exit. Fox takes the street-facing position, and Gordon escorts the worker clear. Keep the encounter brief and credible rather than inventing a spectacular rescue or a named defeated enemy.

**What unsettles Fox:** Gordon is in the exposed doorway with the witness, not directing others from safety. He listens to a useful warning even while refusing Fox's preferred approach.

**What unsettles Gordon:** Fox can act decisively without demanding an audience. Once he understands the worker's situation, he holds the useful position instead of chasing an opportunity to prove himself.

Draft exchange after the immediate danger passes:

> Fox: “You could have said you were moving someone.”
>
> Gordon: “You could have asked before choosing the direction.”
>
> Fox: “Would you have told me?”
>
> Gordon: “Enough to keep you out of the doorway.”

**End:** They have cooperated once, not become friends. Return to the current Blackhaven scene with an economical position check that would have prevented this first misunderstanding.

### FG-F02 — An introduction after the noise: respect becomes a second meeting

**Same historical incident, later:** Away from the worker, they compare what each actually saw. Gordon gives his name and role; Fox gives his name and a concise account. Gordon separates Fox's observation from his inference in the notes. Fox initially hears this as an attempt to make his contribution disappear.

Gordon reads the distinction back: the observation is credited, and the conclusion still needs support. He also records that Fox adjusted to protect the exit. He will not invent certainty, but he will not erase inconvenient help either. Fox points out that Gordon's route check did not account for the lane's blind bend; Gordon adds that omission without changing the subject to Fox's manners.

**First boundary:** Gordon will listen to actionable information and tell Fox what can safely be shared. He will not disclose the worker's destination or private details to prove goodwill. Fox does not get broad clearance, access to protected files or a licence to decide case outcomes through this encounter.

**Reason to meet again:** Fox offers to show Gordon how delivery traffic changes the usable routes through the street. Gordon agrees to a later walk and arrives as arranged. Fox expected an assistant or an excuse; Gordon expected Fox might not show. Both do. Treat this follow-through as a distinct brief time transition within the memory, not an instant friendship montage.

During that walk Gordon asks about a physical detail he missed, and Fox answers without performing expertise. They discover that the other can tolerate a correction. An understated joke about the doorway lets the conversation become less defensive.

**Trust gained:** Fox has evidence that Gordon keeps an appointment, credits useful work and admits a limitation. Gordon has evidence that Fox can protect a confidence and make a contribution that is not simply force. Neither yet trusts the other's judgement in every situation.

**Present return:** After the first current case report, Fox offers to accompany Gordon to check the rail lead before Gordon asks. That small voluntary choice is the payoff, not a speech declaring loyalty.

### FG-F03 — The trail they did not follow: a real cost, an unresolved argument

**Historical situation:** During a later limited operation, a potentially relevant carrier moves away while a consenting witness becomes exposed to an immediate, observable threat. Neither partner can guarantee what the carrier holds. Their available positioning does not allow a safe pursuit and protection at the same time.

Gordon decides to protect the witness and calls Fox back. Fox does return, but resents how little explanation he receives. The carrier gets away; the witness is protected. Neither later discovers a convenient fact proving that the lost lead was worthless or all-important.

**The argument:** Fox believes Gordon makes him useful only when his instincts agree with the plan. Gordon believes Fox is treating a possible future victim as more real than the person currently in danger. Neither accusation is entirely fair. Fox was thinking about what another lost lead might permit, not dismissing the witness; Gordon was making an urgent choice, not treating Fox as disposable.

> Fox: “You asked me to watch the route. Then you asked me to let it go.”
>
> Gordon: “Yes. I needed you back.”
>
> Fox: “Then say that. Don't tell me it stopped mattering.”
>
> Gordon: “It mattered. I chose the person we could still reach.”

Let the conversation stop before perfect agreement. On their next collaboration Gordon states what he needs and what remains uncertain. Fox states when he is about to leave his assigned position rather than assuming Gordon can infer his plan.

**Agreement earned:** An urgent safety call receives an immediate response; its reasoning can be challenged afterward. Whoever changes the plan explains the change when time permits. Neither retrospectively pretends the abandoned objective had no value. This is a coordination practice, not authority to compel the player or excuse any order.

**Present return:** During the contractor threat in chapter 4, they explicitly divide protection and observation before acting. Their improved coordination is visible, but it does not guarantee combat victory.

### FG-F04 — A report with both names: trusting someone to tell the truth about you

**Historical situation:** In a later minor joint operation, Gordon's original briefing relies on an access assumption that proves wrong. Fox spots the change, moves to check another approach and reports that move too late. Their positions temporarily cease to support each other; they regroup and withdraw from that attempt. Do not add a death, permanent injury or dramatic betrayal to manufacture importance.

**Before the debrief:** Fox expects Gordon to protect the written plan and describe him as the sole cause. Gordon expects Fox to refuse any account in which following his own judgement had a cost. Both initially prepare to defend themselves.

**The reversal:** Gordon puts the faulty briefing assumption in his own account before asking for Fox's statement. He does not excuse Fox's delayed communication. Fox then confirms the delay in his account without pretending Gordon ordered the move. Their reports distinguish observation, decision and consequence; neither falsifies anything to protect the other.

> Fox: “That makes your plan look bad.”
>
> Gordon: “That part of it was bad.”
>
> Fox: “And I left you guessing.”
>
> Gordon: “Put that in too.”

**Repair:** They agree on a simple acknowledgement before separating when conditions allow, and a fallback meeting point if communication fails. No newly invented radio system or gameplay capability is required by the outline. The important change is that both now volunteer information the other needs.

After the report, they return to ordinary maintenance. One passes the other a tool without being asked. Keep the gesture small: they are choosing to work together again, not announcing that all conflict is over.

**Trust gained:** Fox trusts Gordon not to erase him, scapegoat him or conceal responsibility behind rank. Gordon trusts Fox to report an inconvenient fact, return when needed and own a mistake. Both know trust permits accurate criticism; it is not a promise to endorse every choice.

**Present return:** In Night Ledger's briefing they name responsibilities and uncertainty clearly. After the defeat, the same honesty makes the argument sharper: Fox expects Gordon to say they lost, and Gordon can accept responsibility without pretending Fox feels nothing. The callback supports the existing ending rather than predicting or preventing it.

### What their partnership looks like by the campaign opening

They respect competence first, then dependability, then the ability to disagree without being abandoned or misrepresented. Fox has learned that Gordon's caution can be an active way of protecting a person; Gordon has learned that Fox's impatience can contain information he has not yet noticed. Neither trait is universally correct.

They have learned some practical habits: short position checks, naming uncertainty, distinguishing an observation from its meaning, explaining changed priorities, and returning to an unfinished argument after immediate danger passes. Their humour can refer to the doorway or a badly phrased report without trivialising frightened witnesses. Each notices when the other is unusually silent, but asks rather than reading his mind.

This history does not require identical beliefs. Fox still distrusts institutions and is willing to use force; Gordon still exercises judgement about force and retains responsibility for his decisions. Their bond is demonstrated by keeping promises, protecting confidences, accepting correction and showing up for unglamorous work. Their first genuinely personal trust is knowing that disagreement does not make the other stop listening.
