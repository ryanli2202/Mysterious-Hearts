## Chapter 1 — The people still at the table

### GD01 — One task at a time [GS01]

April moved a stack of paper away from the edge of the table before Garrett could put another cup on it.

“I knew that was there,” he said.

“I know. I was worried about the cup.”

He looked at the space she had cleared. “You think I'm a mess.”

“I think you've started three things and finished none of them.”

“That's a system.”

“Then tell me which part I'm helping with.”

He almost said Charles. It would have been unfair, asking her to take a name and turn it into a task she could complete.

April waited. She had known him long enough to recognise the joke he was considering and long enough not to rescue him by laughing before he made it.

“I need to sort these,” he said at last. “The things I've actually checked. The things I keep going back to because I want them to be different.”

“Do you want me to read them?”

“Just the ones I hand you.”

“All right.”

She pulled a chair closer. He gave her a page, then took it back to look at the date again.

“You can keep that one for a moment,” she said.

“I know.”

“You don't have to do this quickly for me.”

That stopped him. He had been moving fast enough to pretend he knew what he was doing.

**Accept practical help:** He passed her the page again and explained what he could actually verify. She asked questions without supplying the missing answers.

**Choose an ordinary break:** “Can we do something else first?” he asked. April closed the space between the piles without reading them. “Yes. Pick something small.”

His hand moved toward his phone before he remembered there was no new reason to check it. April noticed, but did not comment. The habit brought back a night when he had been unable to put it down at all.

### GD02 — When the calls stopped [garrett-april-silence]

**FLASHBACK — Golden Dragon after closing, after Charles disappeared.**

The ringing had ended. Garrett kept the phone against his ear for another second, as though the silence might change its mind.

April set something on the table.

“How long have you been sitting here?”

“I checked work again.”

“That wasn't what I asked.”

“No one's heard anything.” He lowered the phone. “It rings. That's the worst part. It sounds like he could pick up.”

April sat opposite him. “Have you eaten?”

“I can't think about dinner.”

“Then don't. I brought something.”

He looked at the bowl and felt an unreasonable flash of irritation. There was food in the world, and people could still decide when to eat it, while he had lost the ability to make one conversation happen.

“I'm not hungry.”

“You don't have to finish it.”

“April.”

She left the bowl where it was. “I'm not trying to fix this with dinner.”

He looked away. “I know.”

For a while she let him be quiet.

“He made such a thing of coming home,” Garrett said. “Of telling someone where you were. He could turn it into a whole lecture.”

“You believed him.”

“I still do. That's the problem. I keep finding reasons it can't be his choice.”

“We don't know what happened.”

“What if he needed something? What if he called at the wrong time, or I missed something he said?”

April put both hands on the table, close to her own bowl.

“I can't answer that by inventing something you should have noticed.”

He swallowed. “First Mum and Dad. Then every time this goes quiet, I'm sixteen again.”

April's face changed, and he hated for a moment that she could see how frightened he was.

“You don't have to make that sound smaller with me,” she said.

“If I stop looking, even for tonight—”

He could not finish it without hearing how little sense it made.

“You'll still be his brother tomorrow,” she said. “We can write down who you've spoken to. What they actually know. We don't have to do all of it while you haven't eaten.”

“You can't promise he's coming back.”

“No.”

The honesty hurt. It also left him nothing he needed to argue against.

“Can you stay?”

“Yes.”

“Until I can put this down.”

April nodded. He turned the phone face down but kept it within reach. She did not ask him to move it farther away.

### GD03 — Dumpling inspection [GR-G01]

The argument was familiar enough to be comfortable. Beneath it, Garrett remembered the stranger with the sketchbook, and how unexpectedly pleased he had been when that stranger came back.

### GD04 — The split dumpling [GR-F01; shared history]

**FLASHBACK — Golden Dragon's early months; Rei arrives alone after a modelling shoot. External dialogue matches Rei RD04.**

Garrett noticed the open seam as soon as he set the plate down. He had meant to notice before carrying it across the room.

“Give me that one. I'll replace it.”

“It's all right.”

“It's opened.”

“I can see that.”

“Is that a complaint?”

“It has more character than the intact ones.”

“That sounds suspiciously like a polite complaint.”

“I ate it.”

“A committed critic.”

“What for?”

“To remember the standard I'm being held to.”

“It isn't a standard.”

“Good. I was worried I'd have to break one on every plate.”

“You don't have to display it,” Rei said.

“I know.”

Garrett returned to work. He noticed that the customer stayed to finish slowly, and made an effort to remember the order.

Garrett leaned against the nearest available surface. “Can you stay a while?”

### GD05 — A lead small enough to doubt [G1; Mei's delivery slip]

Mei turned the old dispatch photograph so Garrett could see the correction.

“Six to four,” she said. “Here.”

He saw the number, then the letter beside it. The crooked H reached him before he had decided what he was looking for.

Charles used to leave notes on the fridge. Garrett remembered being annoyed by how often they contained something he already knew: food, a time, something to remember before going out. Now a shape in a photograph made his chest tighten.

“That's his writing.”

Mei did not agree quickly enough.

“It looks like it,” Garrett corrected himself.

“I thought you should see it.”

“Why didn't—” He stopped. “Sorry. I don't mean that.”

She left the photograph between them.

The date was eleven days after Charles had left his job. Garrett checked it again, trying not to build a journey out of one correction.

“If it's him, he was doing something ordinary,” he said. “Changing a number.”

“It tells us somebody corrected the count.”

“I know what it tells us.” His voice had sharpened. He took a breath. “I know. I'm trying to get the rest of me to keep up.”

Mei nodded. He was grateful she did not ask him to be calmer before continuing.

“The stamp's cut off,” he said.

“There is a complete receiving copy.”

He looked at the photograph once more. The resemblance did not become proof because it hurt to leave it uncertain.

## Chapter 2 — What a record can prove

### GD06 — Mei remembers the working day [GS02; receiving copy]

Garrett took a stack of clean things from Mei when she indicated where they belonged. He needed something to do with his hands while she found the copy.

“Is that helping,” she asked, “or are you going to move everything back afterward?”

“I can do useful work while waiting.”

“Good. Put those there.”

He did. When she laid the record down, he nearly abandoned the last item halfway to its place. He finished moving it first.

The full stamp named West Pier Cold Storage in Hong Kong. Four crates received. No driver signature.

Garrett looked at the space where a name might have been.

“You don't remember who took them?”

“Not enough to give you a name.”

“A description?”

“I don't want to give you something you'll spend a week trying to turn into Charles.”

He looked up, stung, then saw how carefully she was choosing the words.

“You think I'd do that.”

“I think you're hoping. I would rather disappoint you honestly than send you after a person I can't remember.”

He pressed his lips together. “All right.”

Mei pointed out what the receiving record could support. Garrett followed the dates and copied the details he was entitled to keep.

“Thank you,” he said afterward.

“For the paper?”

“For not saying you remember when you don't.”

He went back to the item he had almost dropped. The next place to look was the duplicate tally, not an imagined driver with his brother's face.

### GD07 — What the records cannot say [G2; reconcile the crates]

Two crates had remained at dispatch.

Garrett read the tally again. Six packed, four collected. The correction accounted for the difference. It did not hide a passenger in the arithmetic.

He had not realised how much of a story he had attached to the missing two until the paper took it away.

“Four,” he said under his breath.

An entirely sensible number. He disliked it for a moment.

He pictured Charles looking over his shoulder and telling him to read what was actually there. Then he was angry that he could imagine his brother correcting him more readily than answering the phone.

Garrett moved his notes apart. On one side, the date and delivery acceptance. On the other, the question of who had authorised collection.

**Response: separate packed and collected totals.** He wrote the two quantities beside their actual labels. It felt pedantic until he saw how easily he had been treating them as the same thing.

**Response: record uncertainty.** He wrote that the discrepancy proved neither Charles's authorship nor his travel. He left room for the answer to arrive later instead of forcing one into the line.

He did not have to feel pleased with the correction for it to be useful. Someone had still ordered the collection. Finding out who was a question a record might answer.

When he stood, the disappointment went with him. So did the next question.

### GD08 — A question worth asking [GS03; Dana at the public desk]

Dana let Garrett finish explaining before she moved the receiving record closer.

“This establishes delivery acceptance,” she said.

“And the date.”

“Yes.”

“But not who went with it.”

“Not from this record.”

He looked at the stamp. “I keep bringing people something that almost answers me.”

Dana paused. “It may help to make the next question smaller.”

“My brother is missing. It's not a small question.”

“No. But this document cannot answer all of it.”

He leaned back, hearing the impatience in his own voice too late.

“Sorry.”

“You can ask about the dispatch authorisation. Who ordered the collection, on what date, under which reference.”

“And if his name is there?”

“Then you have the information the authorisation actually records. You check it against the other material.”

He gave a short laugh without much humour. “You don't leave a lot of room to jump ahead.”

“I'm trying to give you somewhere you can step.”

Garrett looked at her properly. She had not promised an investigation, private access or a result. She had listened and given him a question he could use.

“Show me the procedure,” he said, or requested the public form according to the selected response.

He wrote down the requirement for a dated authorisation. The public desk remained a public desk. Leaving with something limited and usable felt better than leaving with a reassurance he could not test.

### GD09 — Dinner without a report [GS04; April]

April took one look at the papers beside Garrett's plate.

“Are those eating with us?”

He moved them. “They don't order much.”

“They occupy a lot of table.”

He put them away, then began to explain the tally.

April waited until he noticed she had not asked.

“You don't want to hear it?”

“I will, if you want to tell me. I also came to have dinner with you.”

He looked down at the food. “I don't know what else I've got to say.”

“You could ask me something.”

That embarrassed him more than a rebuke would have.

“How was your day?”

“Do you want the version where I say fine and we go back to your papers?”

“No.” He put his hands flat beside the plate. “The actual version.”

April studied him, then smiled a little. “I wanted an evening that wasn't organised around fixing something.”

“And I started with freight records.”

“You did.”

“I can be charming without a document.”

“I remember. That's why I keep trying this.”

He laughed. She asked him to pass something, and for a while they discussed the meal, something ordinary he had done badly and something he thought he had done very well. She did not agree with his assessment of the second.

Later, when his attention drifted, she touched the edge of the table rather than his papers.

“Still here?”

“Trying.”

“That's enough. You don't need to be entertaining for two people.”

He took another bite before answering. He wanted her to have an evening with him in it, not just a place beside his search.

### GD13 — What a rumour can do [separate evidence from rumour]

The returned reference mentioned blue and red research files.

Garrett read it beside the street rumour. The rumour had an answer for every missing part. Everyone associated with the office became a dealer. Every unexplained movement became evidence of the same thing.

He could see why it travelled more easily than a careful sentence full of limits.

**Document route:** He compared the references across the retained copies. They were consistent. The labels did not establish what Charles sold, whether he had been abducted, or why he had left. His sealed enquiry had reached the office. That was the actual change.

He pictured repeating the rumour to April or Rei and hearing it become more convincing in his own voice. He put it aside before that happened.

**Rumour-as-proof route:** The selected accusation produces the existing unverified result and next-day retry. Garrett does not receive a confession merely by speaking the claim confidently. His notes preserve what he chose, including the setback, without turning the rumour into historical fact.

For a moment he was angry with Charles for making him do this: separate paper from hope, hope from suspicion, suspicion from things he would hate to have said if his brother walked through the door tomorrow.

Then he folded the verified reference carefully. Anger was something he could admit. It did not need a false charge attached to it.

## Chapter 4 — A question only family can answer

For a moment he resented that too. He had come this far and was still being asked to prove something.

Then he remembered the kitchen, the effort of trying to make a familiar taste arrive through ingredients neither brother trusted himself to measure correctly.

### GD17 — The chipped teacup [G3; new family memory]

**FLASHBACK — An earlier attempt to recreate their mother's broth. Exact date remains unspecified; do not assign this to a final conversation or invent details of their parents' deaths.**

Charles looked at the chipped cup in Garrett's hand.

“You could use something easier to measure with.”

“I'm measuring with it.”

“That's not the same thing.”

“It is if I keep using the same one.”

Charles gave him the look that usually meant the argument had become more work than doing it himself. Garrett held onto the cup.

“I've got this part.”

“All right.”

The concession surprised him enough that he nearly spilled it anyway.

They tasted what they had made and looked away from each other at almost the same time.

“Not quite,” Charles said.

“You don't have to make it sound like a school report.”

“What would you like me to say?”

“That it tastes wrong.”

Charles put down the spoon. “It tastes wrong.”

Garrett had asked for the words. Hearing them made his eyes sting.

He busied himself with the cup.

“I thought I'd know,” he said. “Once it was there. I thought I'd be able to tell what was missing.”

Charles leaned against the counter. “I thought I would too.”

For a while neither tried to fix the recipe.

Garrett turned the cup until the chip faced away from his thumb. “I remember her making it look easy.”

“I don't think we were paying attention to the difficult parts.”

“We were paying attention to eating it.”

Charles's mouth moved into a tired smile. “You were very committed.”

Garrett laughed, then had to wipe at his face. He did not like doing both at once.

“Don't throw it out,” he said when Charles reached for the pot.

“I wasn't going to.”

“We can still eat it.”

“Yes.”

“And try again.”

Charles nodded. Garrett left their father's cup beside the other things, without announcing why he wanted it there.
