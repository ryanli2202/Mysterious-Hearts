## Confirmed direction and campaign-wide correction

The Crow adopted Camila from an orphanage, raised her himself and made her his personal protégée. He has deliberately moulded her to become both his financial Ace and his successor. These are established premises, not a rumour, a reward she might receive from a relationship score or an optional backstory branch.

He genuinely intends her to succeed him. Do not invalidate that premise with a decoy heir, false adoption, secretly unrelated guardian, discarded experiment or an ending revealing that she was never meant to inherit anything. His ambition can include shaping what continues after him without establishing illness, impending death, retirement or a current transfer of power.

The user also confirmed that **the Crow can appear outside Syndicate headquarters in campaigns** and that **he personally defeats Elias at Elias's family crime headquarters**. Those instructions supersede the earlier location restriction for authored campaign scenes. They apply across all Ace campaigns, including Camila's upbringing and present scenes. Exact locations, informed audiences, travel and saved scene context still need authoring.

This correction permits campaign placement; it does not automatically make the Crow a publicly identifiable resident or enable unrestricted everyday invitations. His face, birth identity, age and unrelated private history remain unestablished. Camila's adoption and upbringing are now explicitly known writer facts, so a blanket prohibition on all Crow family history cannot erase them.

## The central relationship

For the other Aces, service begins after another life. Camila's life has been formed inside the Crow's care and expectations. He is the person who gave her a home, taught her how to think about resources and people, and repeatedly described a future large enough to contain her ambitions.

**Proposed emotional foundation:** she loves him as her adoptive father, respects him as her teacher and believes that carrying his work forward gives her life a coherent purpose. Her loyalty is intertwined with family, identity and the desire to be worthy of the trust he has placed in her. Gratitude contributes, but treating adoption as an invoice would flatten the relationship.

His care and his design for her coexist. Show time he actually spent raising her, consistent routines, attention and pride, alongside selective lessons and pressure to become what he intended. The precise quality of his affection is proposed character work; adoption alone neither proves unconditional tenderness nor requires every caring act to be a trick. His ambition, cruelty and desire for control remain real.

She understands more of his institution than an outsider and has knowingly helped it operate. This is not a campaign in which an innocent daughter discovers that her father has a criminal job. The difficult discovery is how much her own judgement has been shaped around preserving his power—and whether she can make decisions he never supplied an answer for.

**Central question:** can a person raised to continue someone else's rule develop the judgement required to exercise that rule herself?

The proposed answer keeps her loyal while making her authorship of decisions visible. A successor needs more than flawless imitation. She can disagree about method, admit uncertainty, accept responsibility and learn from other people without renouncing the Crow or converting the Syndicate into a benevolent institution.

## Childhood and education

Exact adoption age, orphanage name/location, birth-family circumstances and dates remain undecided. Use a childhood adoption with years of direct upbringing before adult service. Her present age of 30 does not establish the Crow's age or a civil identity under which he acted. ### The orphanage and adoption

Give young Camila interests and habits before the Crow appears. She may carefully organise a shared game, bargain over a turn or notice a missing part, but she is a child rather than a miniature CFO. Staff and other children have ordinary personalities; the orphanage need not be abusive, a Syndicate front or a source of secretly engineered trauma.

The Crow sees the potential for the person he means to raise. Exactly what first drew his attention is a proposal to settle, not a surprise that negates the successor premise. His choice should be followed by the actual work of parenting: he returns, she leaves in his care, and an unfamiliar home becomes a continuing relationship.

### Being raised, not merely recruited young

Include scenes with no operational objective: a meal where she is allowed to leave a question unfinished, a lesson interrupted by a childhood frustration, him returning when he said he would, or her showing him something she made. These proposed moments make later financial training and authority emotionally legible. They need not expose his face or narrate his private thoughts.

Later education includes accounting, institutional incentives, negotiation, history, observation and understanding why people cooperate. Her interests in chess and beautiful stationery may appear naturally. Tango and friendship should also offer experiences she values for herself; every preference does not need to have been assigned by him.

1. **Daughter:** the Crow adopted and raised her. This is an established private relationship.
2. **Protégée:** he personally educated her and continues to develop her judgement.
3. **Ace of Diamonds:** her current specialist role is earned and operational; it does not make her commander of the Diamonds Suit.
4. **Intended successor:** he is preparing her to inherit his leadership. Intent does not itself enact a present handover, change ranks or give her unrestricted authority over Sylvanis, Ivan or the other Aces.

## Proposed present conflict: an inheritance must function

A real disruption spreads through a linked set of Syndicate financial commitments, affiliates and legitimate-facing operations. A senior counterparty is trying to detach useful institutions from the Crow's control by persuading their leaders that only his personal authority keeps the arrangements viable.

The opponent's assertion is personal to Camila: she is an excellent accountant raised to execute another person's decisions, and the organisation would never follow her judgement. The Crow responds by giving her a bounded mandate to resolve the crisis across several functions. He remains active and available for defined reports; his absence from individual scenes is neither death nor secret incapacity.

**The crisis is real.** The Crow did not fabricate every loss, place every enemy or endanger employees merely to manufacture an examination. He recognises an opportunity to test and develop his intended successor within an actual threat. His support and the limits of her mandate are explicit.

Proposed supporting cast, names provisional:

- **Ansel Vey, senior financial counterparty:** helped administer a network now tied to the Syndicate, knows particular agreements and wants to control them himself. He judges Camila through the narrow role in which he has dealt with her. He is capable but fallible and has no automatic knowledge of her adoption or successor status.
- **Mara Esteve, former household tutor:** remembers the child and parts of the education, with observations distinct from both Camila's and the Crow's. She can care about Camila without demanding defection or knowing all the family's secrets. Inclusion, chronology and access remain to be locked.
- **Inez Salcedo, affiliate operations manager:** has to keep people paid and essential operations working while distant leaders dispute control. She brings concrete consequences into discussions and retains her own judgement about Camila's proposed arrangements. She is neither an automatic rebel nor an unquestioning beneficiary.

This is a fictional institutional conflict, represented through authored evidence, negotiations, resource choices and in-person encounters. Do not implement real financial advice, fraud instructions or an unrestricted trading simulator. Records establish specific commitments and dependencies; Analysis can reveal details and plausible branches, not an unseen mastermind.

### Escalation and climax

An apparently balanced account conceals a mismatch between reported resources and what a named operation can actually access. Camila identifies the mismatch, follows corroborating testimony and learns that separate affiliates have been offered incompatible promises. Some are exploited, some opportunistic and some simply frightened by the disruption.

She initially tries to find a single correct calculation. The other Aces provide information her records cannot: physical readiness, what was actually observed and how a message has been received. She must choose between viable plans with different costs, explain the choice to people whose cooperation matters and carry it through under opposition.

The final sequence combines a private negotiating session, retrieval/protection of the necessary records or personnel, and a committed operational decision. She uses Analysis and supported combat where needed; administrative authority does not grant automatic physical victory.

Both recommended plans preserve the Crow's stated objective but distribute costs differently. One contains immediate losses by relinquishing a limited asset or relationship; another retains that asset through a larger short-term commitment and a narrower reserve for later needs. Define concrete fictional resources, affected people and feasible alternatives before implementation. There is no secretly costless “smart answer.”

The resolution demonstrates successor-level judgement: she makes the decision, states the uncertainty and accepts responsibility for the people and power affected. The Crow assesses what she did and confirms the seriousness of her preparation. She remains his intended successor; he remains Cardmaster. Formal accession, his death and a global reorganisation are separate future scope.

## Illustrative dialogue anchors

Proposed manuscript lines, not released dialogue or quotations from source:

**When the intended succession is made explicit in the memory:**

> Camila: “You taught me how to keep it running.”  
> Crow: “I have been preparing you to decide where it goes.”

**A present conversation about their relationship:**

> Camila: “Was there ever a lesson you gave me simply because I was your daughter?”  
> Crow: “You were my daughter when I taught you. You remain so when you fail to give me the answer I wanted.”

This is proposed private portrayal, to be assessed against the full manuscript. Its weight must come from actual childhood scenes and continued consequences, not one line declaring him a harmless parent.

**Before the climax:**

> Camila: “I can defend either plan. I cannot promise either one will spare everyone.”  
> Crow: “Then choose what you are prepared to sustain. Give me the decision and the cost.”

**After a resolved outcome:**

> Camila: “I did not choose it because I thought you would.”  
> Crow: “Tell me why you did.”

The answer is the player's recorded reasoning and the actual result, not an automatically flattering speech.

## Endings, choices and closure

Recommended supported branches:

| Outcome | Required condition | Closure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A decision she can own** | Core interference resolved, chosen plan executed and promised follow-through supported by actual state | She gives a defensible account, acknowledges losses and continues as intended successor. The Crow retains command. |
| **An inheritance with a cost** | Core objective secured with a recorded lost asset, damaged agreement or incomplete secondary recovery | The aftermath names the cost and who bears it. Her father evaluates judgement and follow-through; a single imperfect result does not erase adoption or make the heir premise false. |
| **The account remains open** | Core objective incomplete | Recovery, renegotiation or retry follows. No false campaign completion, duplicated rewards or invented certainty. |

Decisions concern evidence, disclosure, delegation, negotiated terms, priorities and accepted costs. Loyalty is not a morality meter. Family attachment is not a purchasable score. Do not require patricide, abandonment, an unmasking or an immediate coup for the story to conclude.

The ending resolves the present institutional conflict and establishes that her successor preparation has substance. She can love her father, continue his work and have a judgement distinguishable from his. That does not resolve the moral costs of the power she intends to inherit.
