## The central contradiction

Martos joins the military because he believes strength should create dependable safety: someone must stand where other people cannot, keep a promise under pressure and bring the team home. He thrives in disciplined collective work. Special forces give him a place where preparation, judgement and mutual trust matter as much as his size and Trait.

Over successive deployments, those virtues are used to make him participate in abuse. His commanders describe detention as protection, civilian harm as necessity and expendable soldiers as honoured comrades. He objects in private but keeps carrying out enough of the work to make the operations possible. Desertion follows a deliberate breaking point; it does not erase his earlier choices.

He emerges convinced that authority concealed behind virtuous language is especially dangerous. He still believes a strong command is necessary. The Crow offers an identifiable sovereign, explicit objectives, material support and promises he demonstrably keeps. Martos mistakes those qualities for a sufficient answer to the question of who deserves obedience.

**His conviction:** a commander who states what he wants and stands behind the people carrying it out has earned service that a uniform alone cannot demand.

**His distortion:** he comes to judge the Crow's right to command by reliability toward his own people, while treating the people harmed outside that circle as costs somebody must bear. Candour about domination becomes a substitute for legitimacy. His care is real, and so is his willingness to help a cruel organisation.

The campaign explains why this belief becomes emotionally absolute. It does not establish that the belief is correct, that desertion makes him innocent or that the Crow is a better person because he acknowledges his ambition. Martos knowingly serves a criminal ruler; his present loyalty is not a misunderstanding awaiting one revealing document.

### Heinous orders and responsibility

Proposed escalation has three distinct stages:

1. **The cordon:** he participates in forcibly removing civilians from their homes under a purported security measure. His task is to hold the boundary. He sees what that task means to the people trying to leave with relatives and belongings; the narration does not hide the harm inside military euphemisms.
2. **The transfer:** he continues guarding detainees after learning that prisoners have been abused and that the transfer is intended to make them disappear. He objects, accepts his commander's assurances and remains at his post. His involvement is conscious complicity, not a later revelation that absolves everything he thought he was doing.
3. **The disposal order:** the commander orders surviving witnesses killed and the wounded abandoned. Martos refuses, releases the people he can reach and deserts with a wounded colleague. His refusal is late and incomplete. A person he helps can still blame him for earlier actions.

These are specific dramatic proposals to settle before the manuscript. Keep atrocities non-graphic, centre affected people's experience and let survivors retain names, disagreements and independent aims. Do not make cruelty a scored minigame. Historical wrongdoing can be fixed authored history with reflection and aftermath choices; playable action belongs in service exercises, protection, withdrawal and escape. Refusing a later order must not silently rewrite earlier participation.

### Special-forces experience on screen

Show professional habits through decisions and relationships: concise handovers, admitting uncertainty, noticing exhaustion, sharing responsibility, adapting when the plan fails and choosing the objective over a needless fight. Use fictional mission objectives and the existing game systems rather than real operational instruction.

Give the team a shared meal, a disagreement after a rescue and a moment when someone else catches Martos's mistake. His dry humour and practical care predate recruitment. His watch repair and cooking can recur as ordinary pleasures; they do not all need traumatic origin stories. Formal unit jargon and weapon detail cannot substitute for the people who made him feel he belonged.

### Worldview in ordinary life and conflict

- **Promises must be measurable.** He gives an actual time, brings the promised meal and repairs a loose chair. In operations he respects an order that names its objective and cost. This makes the Crow's selective follow-through persuasive.
- **Strength creates obligations.** He takes the difficult position and hates spending another person's safety. He can also decide, paternalistically, that he knows which risks others should be allowed to take.
- **Belonging is earned through service.** He fears being useful only until he fails. Special forces and later the Aces give him a place where competence and mutual reliance feel like family.
- **Someone must decide.** After desertion, uncertainty becomes evidence to him that leaderless freedom cannot protect people. The story gives other characters room to challenge that conclusion without making them naive.
- **Hypocrisy is intolerable.** He treats the Crow's admitted ambition as more trustworthy than his former commander's denials. His blind spot is equating stated intent with justified authority.
- **Failure is personal.** He tries to absorb every loss and conceal strain. Accepting help improves his conduct without automatically dismantling his allegiance.

## Rage: expertise, fear and ongoing limits

Rage is an established Trait with physical costs, distinct from ordinary anger, military discipline and traumatic recollection. Do not turn every angry remark into involuntary transformation or diagnose a mental health condition through a power mechanic. Its original Awakening circumstances remain undecided; abuse did not grant it as a reward.

**Military use:** his commander increasingly treats rising output as an expendable resource. In one proposed mission, Martos pushes past safe control to finish an extraction. Reduced judgement and coordination cause a preventable injury to his teammate. This is one event within his history, not the explanation for every crime committed by the unit.

**His own responsibility:** he learns to recognise rising pressure, communicate it and accept a handover before precision fails. Training scenes include a failed attempt and an honest debrief. The Crow provides resources and enforces operational limits; Martos and his colleagues do the work of maintaining control.

**Player expression:** supported choices should make protection, positioning, restraint, resource management and a timely handover valuable alongside damage. Tie every effect to actual encounter state. If the current engine cannot represent a proposed control action, specify and verify it before writing prose that claims it occurred. No instant cooling button, permanent cure, unlimited growth or guaranteed victory.

**Final payoff:** the former commander tries to provoke an uncontrolled pursuit. Martos completes the promised protection and trusts a colleague with part of the task instead of abandoning it to prove his strength. The fight can demand Rage; the achievement is using force within its limits and accepting support. Quiet habits and another person's voice may help him focus, but never switch his Trait off by affection alone.

## Present-day cast and conflict

New names are provisional and belong only to this proposed route:

- **Lena Varo — survivor and civilian logistics coordinator.** She survived the unit's coercive operation and remembers Martos holding the cordon. She now has an authenticated account linking its former command network to a security consortium operating in Winterfield. Her aim is to get herself and the agreed records to an independently chosen recipient. She accepts limited protection terms because the immediate threat is real; she understands that the Syndicate has its own interests. She decides what she shares personally and owes Martos neither forgiveness nor comfort.
- **Sera Dalm — former team medic.** Wounded during the desertion, she received the support the Crow promised. She values that help without adopting Martos's loyalty. Her present participation must be voluntary and medically/chronologically consistent with the eventual history. She remembers both his care and his failures; she cannot serve as proof that everyone he hurt forgave him.
- **Adrien Voss — former commanding officer.** Former rank and current title remain undecided. He now manages the consortium's armed work and contractual relationships. His objective is to preserve that organisation and prevent authenticated testimony from reaching its recipient. He can rationalise his choices as institutional necessity, recognise Martos's competence and exploit shared history. He neither controls all military institutions nor gains automatic access to the Crow.

The Crow wants the consortium's interference ended and its valuable connections exposed to Syndicate pressure. He authorises keeping Lena alive and delivering the agreed evidence because it weakens a rival. He also demands an accurate private report for his own advantage. Lena's safe passage can therefore succeed while the Crow gains power; the campaign does not frame the entire Syndicate as a rescue organisation.

Unlike Mavis's proposed former-handler plot, this is an escort/protection story carrying someone else's accusation, including accusations against its protagonist. Martos already knows what he did. The current investigation establishes who is acting now and which parts of the old command network still operate, rather than revealing that his entire past was secretly innocent.

## Choices, endings and consequences

Record specific decisions, actual combat outcomes and recipient-scoped disclosures. Avoid reducing this story to a virtue score or a generic trust threshold.

- **Protection agreement:** Lena can accept or reject proposed arrangements. Provide a viable negotiated route and a clear delay/fallback if terms are not yet agreed. She is not cargo the player can relocate without explanation.
- **Account of the past:** reflection can change how directly Martos accepts responsibility, what he asks Sera and which private details he shares. It cannot convert established complicity into innocence.
- **Rage and support:** use observable combat limits and agreed support. Communicating strain or delegating changes the actual plan; optional social scenes must not hide the only viable control mechanism.
- **Evidence:** preserve authenticity, chain of possession within the fictional plot and agreed recipients. Any optional disclosure beyond the mission needs a real consent/knowledge decision; no silent editing to remove his own conduct.
- **Final priority:** choices affect extraction cost, support and the possibility of securing Voss. The required protection order remains the objective. Voss's words do not force a berserk loss in a cutscene.

Recommended endings to specify and implement:

| Outcome | Condition | Closure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The promise kept** | Lena reaches her agreed recipient; the agreed records arrive intact; Voss's current operation is stopped; capture/handoff succeeds through supported systems | Her immediate threat is resolved. Martos accepts his place in the record and the help he received. The Crow gains strategic advantage. Investigation beyond the handoff remains ongoing rather than an invented verdict. |
| **Delivered at a cost** | Core delivery and protection succeed, with a recorded support, resource or secondary capture failure | End the active operation and explain the exact residual risk. Sera/Lena/colleagues respond to what happened. Do not describe Voss as captured if he escaped or turn all injury into cosmetic prose. |
| **Withdrawal and recovery** | Required delivery or protection is incomplete | Continue through a truthful recovery or checkpoint. Preserve paid costs and earned progress; no campaign-complete badge or duplicated rewards. |

No completed ending requires renouncing the Crow. A future defection route would change existing character canon and is outside this requested treatment. Closure comes from resolving this protection mission and Martos taking a more honest view of his own actions, while retaining a deeply compromised loyalty.
