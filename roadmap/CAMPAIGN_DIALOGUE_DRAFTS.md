## Writing and presentation contract

- Expand the exchange through emotional movement: what the character hopes to hear, what they actually hear, resistance or misunderstanding, a more difficult admission, and what they can do next. Give another character room to respond before advancing the subject.
- Use concrete shared details, unfinished ordinary tasks, gestures, pauses and close-third interiority. People may repeat a phrase because its meaning has changed, but do not restate the same argument merely to add words.
- Let supporters struggle for words, have feelings of their own, and sometimes ask an imperfect question. Do not make every friend a polished counsellor or every opponent a speechmaker. Preserve short-spoken characters by adding responsive exchanges and narration rather than long monologues.
- Longer is not uniformly solemn. Warmth, embarrassment, ordinary irritation, relief and affectionate teasing need the same care as grief. Not every scene must end in a lesson or reconciled disagreement.
- Facts, motives and knowledge remain within established canon and the active save. New sensory/staging details in these drafts are proposed scene writing, not new case evidence or historical revelations. Do not invent final words, methods, a single cause of Marcus's death or the idea that Rei should have known how to prevent it.
- Keep historical memories' external facts fixed. Present-day player choices can affect disclosure, response and pacing without changing established outcomes. Break passages into readable, user-advanced beats with saved position and history; all scenes remain in-person.
- Use the passages below as scale/style references, not minimum word quotas. Major scenes need room to develop; transitions and sparse exchanges can remain short. Full manuscript coverage and chapter-level reading review remain pending.

## Draft D01 — The empty place on the bench

**Mapping:** Rei chapter 3; expands existing `rei-school-news`. Historical setting and cast remain Rei, Emily and Sophie at school. Emily conveys the family's news; no new cause, final message or circumstances of death are supplied. Viewpoint: Rei.

Rei had kept the next storyboard page flat against his notebook all morning. One corner had folded in his bag anyway. He worked at it with his thumb as he crossed the courtyard, thinking Marcus would notice it before he noticed the drawing.

Emily and Sophie were already at the bench.

There was room between them. Neither moved to fill it.

“Where is he?” Rei asked. “I brought the next page.”

Emily looked at the notebook, then back at him. Her mouth opened, but nothing came out.

“What?” He stopped trying to smooth the corner. “Did I get something wrong?”

“Rei,” she said. “Can you sit down with us?”

He looked at Sophie. She was holding her hands together so tightly that he could see the pressure in her fingers.

“Why? What's happened?”

“We didn't want you hearing it from someone in the corridor,” Sophie said.

“Hearing what?”

Emily drew a breath that caught before she could finish it.

“Marcus died.”

For a moment, Rei waited for the rest. There had to be another part of the sentence, something that changed what those two words meant.

Emily wiped at her cheek with the heel of her hand.

“His family told the school. He died by suicide.”

“No.” Rei heard how flat his voice sounded. He tried again. “No, we were going to finish this.”

He held up the notebook. Neither of them looked away from him.

“The film,” he said, because perhaps they hadn't understood. “I've got the next bit. He said the pigeon could—”

He stopped. He could picture Marcus reaching across the page, impatient to change something. He could almost hear the argument they would have about it.

“He still has my pencil.”

Sophie pressed her lips together. Then she moved her bag off the bench, making room for him.

“I keep looking up,” she said. “Every time someone comes round that corner.”

Rei looked too.

A student he barely knew crossed the courtyard. Someone called to someone else near the doors. Nothing stopped.

He sat because his knees had begun to feel unreliable. The notebook stayed upright in his hands until Emily asked, very quietly, “Do you want me to hold that?”

“No.”

“All right.”

He lowered it onto his lap himself.

“Are they sure?” he asked.

Emily nodded. Tears slipped down her face while she tried to keep looking at him.

“I wish I had something different to tell you.”

“Did he say anything? Did anyone—” Rei swallowed. “Did someone know?”

“I don't know,” she said. “I don't know everything that happened.”

He wanted her to keep talking anyway. Any detail might make this into something with an edge he could hold.

“Joseph knew he was hurting him.”

Sophie's eyes closed for a moment.

“He knew,” Rei repeated. “He watched him. They all did. And then they'd laugh like it wasn't anything.”

“What they did was cruel,” Sophie said. Her voice shook. “I'm not going to pretend it wasn't.”

“Then why didn't anybody stop it?”

Neither answered immediately.

Emily looked down at the notebook. “I keep going over things too. What I saw. What I said. What I should have said.” She caught herself and shook her head. “I don't have an answer that makes this make sense.”

Rei rubbed his thumb against the folded corner again. It had softened until it would no longer lie flat.

“I should have stayed with him.”

“Rei—”

“I could have. There were times I could have stayed.”

Emily shifted closer, leaving a little space between them.

“You were his friend,” she said. “You cared about him. That part is true.”

“It didn't help.”

“You don't know that.” Her voice broke. She waited before trying again. “You don't know that being his friend meant nothing to him.”

Rei stared at the page until the lines blurred. The little buildings were still there. The pigeon was still waiting for a bus that made no sense.

“I don't want this to be the only thing I can remember now.”

Sophie wiped her face with her sleeve.

“Then we can talk about the other things,” she said. “When you want to. Not all now.”

“I don't think I can go into class.”

“We can go to the counsellor,” Emily said. “I'll come with you.”

“Me too,” Sophie said.

“Not yet.”

“All right.”

Rei's breathing caught. He tried to hide it by bending over the notebook, but that only brought the drawings closer.

“Can you just stay here?”

Emily settled beside him. “Yes.”

Sophie stayed on his other side. No one reached for the page again.

He could not think further ahead than that: the bench, the notebook, the two of them staying. For a while, they let that be enough to do.

**Present-day return:** Transition explicitly back to adult Rei and the storyboard at home. Do not cut immediately to a travel objective. Let him notice the physical page, put it down or keep it open, and choose whether to accept the planned present-day company. Memory completion alone grants no new investigative evidence and does not resolve grief.

## Draft D02 — The brother behind the door

**Mapping:** Garrett chapter 5; an expanded opening segment of the private laboratory confrontation before its existing fight. It does not replace the victory gate or prematurely complete reconciliation. Viewpoint: Garrett. Preserve the eventual authorised explanation and later boundaries choice.

Charles was standing in front of him.

Garrett had imagined this often enough that he distrusted the first second of it. He looked for something ordinary to confirm it: the angle of Charles's shoulders, the way he went still before choosing what to say.

“You're here,” Garrett said.

“Yes.”

The answer was so small that Garrett almost laughed.

“That's it?”

Charles glanced at the door. “Keep your voice down.”

For months, Garrett had wanted nothing more than to hear that voice. Now he could barely stand it.

“Don't do that.”

“Garrett—”

“Don't start with an instruction. You don't get to disappear and have the first thing you say be something I'm doing wrong.”

Charles looked back at him. “You're right.”

Garrett had been ready for an argument. The admission left him with nowhere to put the next sentence.

“I called you.”

“I know.”

“How much do you know? Do you know I kept thinking there must be some reason you couldn't pick up? Not wouldn't. Couldn't.”

Charles's hands shifted at his sides, then stopped.

“I thought keeping you away from this would keep you safe.”

“You keep saying safe like it tells me where you were.”

“I can't explain this in a corridor.”

“Then explain it here. I'm here.” Garrett spread his hands, angry at how much they were shaking. “You can see that, can't you? I'm not a voice on your phone anymore.”

Charles took a breath. “There are people who could use you to get to me.”

“So you made sure I couldn't get to you either.”

“I was afraid.”

Garrett looked at him. For a moment it was easier to be furious than to hear that.

“So was I,” he said. “Every day. You left me with that, and you weren't even there to be angry at.”

Charles lowered his gaze, briefly.

“I didn't know how to tell you enough without bringing you into it.”

“You could have told me you were alive.”

The words came out quieter than Garrett expected. They hurt more that way.

“I needed my brother,” he said. “I didn't need you to have every answer. I needed you to let me know you were still somewhere in the world.”

Charles looked up again. “I am.”

“I can see that now.” Garrett swallowed. “You don't understand how long it took me to get to now.”

**Continuation:** Let Charles's established protection/control argument and Garrett's demand to be heard develop into the existing authored confrontation. Do not make this admission alone avert the mandatory fight or establish repaired trust. After the actual outcome, the spare-bowl scene carries feelings that neither resolved at the door.

## Draft D03 — Dinner that is only dinner

**Mapping:** GR-G06 in Garrett chapter 10; optional social version of the existing closing meal, or deferred if the player chose privacy. Rei knows only what Garrett has actually shared. Viewpoint: Garrett. This passage requires no private case disclosure.

Rei lifted one of the dumplings from the tray and turned it between his fingers.

Garrett pointed at him with the spoon. “Don't start.”

“I haven't said anything.”

“You've got the expression.”

“What expression?”

“The one where you decide something I've cooked needs a character study.”

Rei looked at the dumpling again. “This one has had a difficult afternoon.”

“It was fine until you picked it up.”

“So was the first one.”

Garrett laughed before he could stop himself. The sound surprised him; he hadn't realised how long he had been standing there without making one.

Rei put the dumpling down gently.

“You still keep it?” he asked.

“Which?”

“The drawing of you stealing one.”

“In the till.” Garrett reached for another wrapper. “Different drawing. Your first complaint was the split seam.”

“It wasn't a complaint.”

“I know that now. You could have made it clearer before I wondered whether you were going to review the whole menu in pictures.”

“You remembered my order.”

“You kept coming back.”

“I didn't always order.”

Garrett looked up. Rei was concentrating on folding a wrapper, giving him room to decide whether to answer that.

“No,” Garrett said. “You didn't.”

For a few moments, the only conversation was the tray moving between them.

Then Garrett said, “I'm glad you did.”

Rei glanced at him. The reply took a moment.

“Me too.”

Garrett pressed the edges of his dumpling together too firmly. A little filling escaped.

Rei looked down at it.

“Don't,” Garrett said.

“I could draw that one.”

“You could put it in the pot.”

Rei laughed and reached for the tray. Garrett let him take it, then asked how his day had been. This time he waited for the whole answer.

**Purpose:** Give affection room without forcing a confession, romantic framing or campaign recap. Preserve the distinction between the first split-dumpling receipt and the later till-drawer sketch.

## Draft D04 — Stop saying almost

**Mapping:** Fox/Gordon chapter 11, FG32, current Crow-defeat branch only. Recovery and speech readiness must be established. Viewpoint: Gordon; Fox's version needs its own interior narration without changing the external dialogue. Inapplicable to legacy captured-Crow saves.

Gordon had read the first sentence three times without changing it.

Fox sat on the other side of the table. He had stopped moving the loose sheet in front of him. Somehow the stillness was harder to ignore than the sound had been.

“What?” Gordon asked.

“You keep stopping at the same place.”

“I'm deciding how to record the outcome.”

“We lost.”

“That is in the report.”

“Then why does it take so long to get there?”

Gordon looked at the page. He had begun with the verified entry, the retained evidence, the team's return. All true. He had chosen each truth carefully enough to postpone another one.

“The sequence matters.”

“So does what you're putting first.”

Gordon set the pen down.

“What do you want me to say?”

Fox's jaw tightened. “Don't ask me to write it for you.”

For a moment Gordon wanted to answer as a commander, to return the conversation to something with a procedure. Fox had been there. He had earned more than that.

“We failed to contain him,” Gordon said. “The investigation gave us his location. I treated that as more of a tactical answer than it was.”

Fox looked down at the loose sheet.

“I went through the door too.”

“I know.”

“Do you? Because you can put your name on every mistake in the room and still avoid talking to me about it.”

Gordon had no immediate answer.

Fox pushed the paper a little farther away.

“I wanted him to be the last one,” he said. “I knew better than to say it like that. Still wanted it.”

“The organisation would have remained.”

“I know.” Fox looked up. “I'm telling you what I wanted. Not asking you to explain the case.”

Gordon nodded once. He left the pen where it was.

After a while he said, “I wanted to bring everyone back with something that justified taking them in.”

“They came back.”

“That doesn't make the decision right.”

“No.” Fox paused. “It doesn't make them an argument for it, either.”

Gordon looked toward the closed files. The four completed enquiries had not changed while they were at headquarters. Neither had the people who had needed their help.

“We keep the findings,” he said. “We record the failure. We don't make one cancel the other.”

Fox rubbed a hand over his face.

“And tomorrow?”

“We check the protected contact route.”

A small nod. Nothing like forgiveness, and Gordon wasn't sure that was what either of them was asking for.

“Put that down after,” Fox said.

“After what?”

“The part where we lost.”

Gordon picked up the pen. This time he changed the first sentence.

**Continuation:** Do not end their disagreement with an instant inspirational vow. Use the existing review of the four files, practical follow-through and Blackhaven return to demonstrate repair over subsequent scenes. No new casualties, recovered command archive or guaranteed rematch.
