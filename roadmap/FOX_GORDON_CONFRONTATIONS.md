## C01 — Martos Cabrera, Ace of Clubs: the man who holds the position

**Placement:** Chapter 5, after FG-C01 guards and the independently supported Clubs reveal; before the existing masked-specialist fight. Follow with FG15 and the actual case review.

**Dramatic centre:** Fox and Gordon face disciplined commitment rather than an uncontrolled brute. Martos respects precise intentions but will not abandon his duty because the investigators are competent.

### Arrival and opening

Martos assesses their actual condition after the guards. He does not need to announce their injuries or abilities unless visible. His measured speech should slow the scene rather than fill it with threats. Gordon states the specific escort-order linkage; Fox watches the route Martos occupies and waits for his response.

> Gordon: “The original order and the deployment establish your role. We are here about this operation.”
>
> Martos: “Then speak about the operation.”
>
> Fox: “You intend to keep us from it.”
>
> Martos: “Yes.”
>
> Gordon: “Stand aside.”
>
> Martos: “No.”

Gordon recognises an opponent making a deliberate decision, not someone misunderstanding the case. Fox dislikes that Martos's restraint resembles the dependable behaviour he respects in Gordon. The prose can let him notice that uncomfortable resemblance without equating their purposes.

### Mutual reactions and escalation

Martos does not taunt Fox's senses or Gordon's command history, which he has no grounds to know. He responds to how they coordinate in front of him. If their visible positioning changes, a short line such as “You are watching the same space” recognises cooperation without reading a plan.

Rage is a pressure-dependent ability, not Martos's default personality. Should an actual activation or escalation occur, his voice may become more economical while physical changes carry the pressure. Do not portray every exchange as berserk shouting. Overmutation limits belong to implemented mechanics and observed state, not an automatic weakness Fox can trigger by insulting him.

Conditional examples:

- After a genuinely resisted attack, Fox: “That held.” Gordon: “Change the question.” This acknowledges a failed approach without awarding the next one success.
- After a real opening caused by overcommitment, Gordon can identify it concisely; the player still chooses and resolves the action.
- If Martos takes a meaningful setback, his response can be “Again,” directed at continuing his duty, rather than a boast of invulnerability.

### Win, loss and aftermath

**Actual team victory:** Give the moment weight. Martos's inability to hold this position is a local failure of the duty he chose. If present and able to speak:

> Martos: “You have this operation. Do not confuse it with all the others.”
>
> Gordon: “We will record exactly what we secured.”
>
> Fox: “And come back for the next thing we can prove.”

The existing case review records operational material and the earned defeat, without permanent custody or wholesale collapse. Fox notices how much effort a single disciplined opponent required. Gordon refuses to let respect for Martos's competence soften the evidence against the operation. Neither needs to claim Martos was secretly good or that his loyalty broke.

**Actual team failure:** Martos remains an unresolved opponent. Retain existing evidence and guard victories according to saved mechanics. A regroup conversation examines their actual ineffective actions rather than inventing a particular misplay or blaming Fox by default.

**Carry-forward:** In later briefings, the partners remember that control and loyalty can make an enemy more dangerous than visible anger. Refer to this encounter only if actually completed.

## C02 — Elias Braun, Ace of Hearts: who gets to frame the room

**Placement:** Chapter 6 after FG-C02; the false-performer attribution has been resolved and the actual reveal earned. Follow with FG18.

**Dramatic centre:** Elias tries to control the terms of the exchange. His social precision is real, but neither mind control nor proof he knows the investigators' private weaknesses. His warmth and expressive voice survive the confrontation without becoming automatic flirtation.

### Opening

Elias notices the contrast between Fox's directness and Gordon's formal account. He attempts to make their accusation sound like an interpretation rather than a supported connection.

> Elias: “You have already decided how this conversation ends.”
>
> Gordon: “We decided which records match. Your response is still yours.”
>
> Elias: “And he has come to provide the punctuation?”
>
> Fox: “I can speak for myself.”
>
> Gordon: “Then answer both of us.”

Fox resents being cast as a weapon beside Gordon. Gordon's reply declines Elias's attempted division without speaking over his partner. Elias observes that the distinction did not separate them and changes his approach rather than repeating the same joke.

### Reactions and combat

Elias may offer a plausible narrow explanation for one observed act, but cannot unmake the independently corroborated record by sounding persuasive. Gordon answers the actual claim, not every rhetorical flourish. Fox can ask one concrete question rather than interrupt with threats.

> Fox: “The instruction reached the people who carried it out. What part are you disputing?”
>
> Elias: “That a document tells you everything about the person holding it.”
>
> Gordon: “It does not have to tell us everything.”

The eventual combat remains the existing refusal/opposition encounter; conversation does not magically secure surrender. Pyromovement builds heat through actual movement. Choreographic descriptions may be expressive, but no unimplemented inferno, automatic dodge or free heat accumulation is invented. His trained martial arts remain relevant at close range.

If the engine actually limits his movement or disrupts a committed action, let a plainer line replace the flourish. Fox notices the change in cadence, not an inner confession. Gordon must participate through the real ally mechanics rather than spending the whole fight narrating Elias's technique.

### Aftermath

**Actual team victory:** Elias tries briefly to regain control of the exchange. Avoid a compulsory joke at the expense of a badly hurt opponent.

> Elias: “You will make this very simple in your report.”
>
> Gordon: “Clear. There is a difference.”
>
> Fox: “The instruction stays in it.”

The report states what the evidence and fight established. No public outing, filming or civilian career destruction automatically follows. In private, Fox acknowledges he nearly let Elias make the conversation about his anger. Gordon acknowledges that responding to every polished objection could have let Elias set the entire agenda. Neither treats this as a victory over personality itself.

**Failure:** Keep the operation unresolved and preserve already-earned identity knowledge. On retry Elias may reference only the actual previous encounter; he cannot know the team's private regroup conversation. Prose must not call a failed exchange a victory merely because they saw through his rhetoric.

**Carry-forward:** Their next confrontation uses shorter statements of purpose and leaves fewer opportunities for someone else to define their partnership.

**Dramatic centre:** Camila is exacting, adaptable and capable of admitting a narrow correction without abandoning her allegiance. She tests assumptions instead of performing emotionless omniscience. She must not be reduced to financial puns.

> Camila: “You have established my involvement. What else do you think that establishes?”
>
> Gordon: “The authorisation in this file. Not every decision made by the organisation.”
>
> Camila: “Good. Keep that distinction when you lose patience.”

Her acknowledgement concerns the case's already-established linkage; the investigators do not need a confession to validate it. Writer direction: she can respect the answer while opposing them. ### Escalation and reactive fight dialogue

> Gordon: “Will you cooperate with the operation?”
>
> Camila: “You have mistaken agreement about a fact for agreement about what follows.”
>
> Fox: “No. We are asking.”

Her refusal leads into the actual confrontation. Analysis reads observable detail and branching possibilities; it does not know a hidden input, the player's thoughts or unseen allies. Correct predictions must follow implemented mechanics and resolved actions, not an unconditional cutscene where she counters everything.

Conditional examples:

- When their changed approach actually succeeds, allow a short acknowledgment—“Better”—rather than an instant claim she planned the setback.
- Gordon can announce a practical reassessment; Fox communicates a chosen movement before acting. Neither receives a guaranteed bonus because the dialogue describes teamwork.

### Aftermath and foreshadowing

**Actual team victory:** Camila accepts the local result without delivering the Crow's secrets.

>
> Gordon: “We secured the material attached to this operation.”
>

She can assess what she observed, not announce that she foresaw every future choice. Their private aftermath distinguishes confidence from prediction: Gordon was drawn to answering every objection; Fox nearly treated revision as weakness until he saw its practical value. The following quiet equipment scene lets each accept ordinary help.

Her warning may later resonate with the Crow confrontation, but the campaign must not imply the investigators had a fully specified prophecy and ignored it. No new Crow ability, identity or inevitable outcome is revealed here.

**Failure:** Record the real result and preserve evidence. Reassessment should identify an actually observed pattern, resource issue or position; if the cause cannot be inferred, acknowledge that instead of inventing a diagnostic explanation.

## C05 — The Crow: an address is not authority

**Placement:** Chapter 10 after scoped HQ entry and FG-C06, then chapters 11–12 recovery and ongoing resistance. The current scripted defeat and legacy capture saves have separate aftermath paths.

**Dramatic centre:** The Crow treats expanded dominion as his entitlement. His controlled delivery is an expression of ambition, not mild managerial caution. Fox and Gordon arrive with a valid investigation; the error is treating that achievement as sufficient basis for containment. They remain competent and are still outmatched in this authored encounter.

### Entry and opening dialogue

Keep the opaque beaked mask, robes and concealed body. Narrate no eyes, skin, age, smile beneath the mask or unmasked physique. He appears only in headquarters. Do not claim he arranged their whole investigation or wanted the four Ace defeats unless such facts are separately established, which they currently are not.

> Gordon: “We traced the command records to this site. We are here to contain the operation.”
>
> The Crow: “You have reached a place I control. You have not acquired that control.”
>
> Fox: “Then we are clear about the disagreement.”
>
> The Crow: “Your reach ends here. Mine will not.”

Fox finds no panic or need for approval to push against. Gordon notices the absence of negotiation over the established location: the Crow disputes their power to impose a result. These are their interpretations of the exchange, not access to his internal thoughts.

### Acknowledging the Aces without a knowledge leak

Gordon may state that the team has independently established all four operational identities and recovered command records, because that is the entry prerequisite. The Crow does not need to know the team's private methods or every earlier conversation.

> Gordon: “Your four specialists were defeated in those operations. Their orders are in our files.”
>
> The Crow: “Their failures do not transfer my authority to you.”
>
> Fox: “People are not something you own.”
>
> The Crow: “You may object. You will not set the limits of what I take.”

Keep this brief and specific. No stock chess metaphors, secret familial links, invented surveillance or a grand explanation of his personal history. The writer-only intellectual lore in `the_crow.py` is not disclosed to the protagonists as a named ability. Combat descriptions use the actually installed abilities and observed behaviour; differing private lore labels do not license a new power here.

### The fight and mutual reactions

The encounter already discloses its authored defeat framing. Preserve that transparency rather than promising a dialogue choice that can win. The pair's actual commands can play out, but do not extend the fight into a newly winnable boss without a separate scope change.

Use brief event-conditioned reactions to demonstrate the gap: a committed action does not produce the expected control, an observed pattern is countered, or maintaining position requires more than they can sustain. None of those examples is unconditional; narration must follow the combat log. The Crow's effective intelligence is shown through observed decisions, not impossible knowledge of controls or future choices.

If the team actually forces an adjustment, acknowledge it without awarding victory or pretending he planned every setback. The Crow can change his approach without losing his ambition or composure. Fox and Gordon can perform competently without the narration claiming they were close to winning.

As the authored withdrawal resolves, Fox keeps the route in sight and Gordon covers retreat. Use the existing resolved extraction, not invented player movement while knocked out. A short exchange may accompany the established outcome if both are able to speak:

> Fox: “The route is still there.”
>
> Gordon: “Go. I am with you.”

Do not have the Crow explicitly spare them, offer recruitment, pursue them to the recovery station or reveal a secret reason for their survival. Those would be new outcome facts. His last line is optional and must not imply unrecorded control over their extraction.

### Immediate aftermath — current defeat route

**Recovery station:** Begin with practical needs and the actual returning team. Gordon checks who is accounted for. Fox refuses to turn the operation into a near-success story. No invented casualty, permanent disability, civilian massacre or new archive recovered during retreat.

> Gordon: “The team is back. The witnesses were never at the site.”
>
> Fox: “Good. Now say the other part.”
>
> Gordon: “We failed to contain him.”
>
> Fox: “We lost.”
>
> Gordon: “Yes.”

Let silence remain. Gordon's concise admission matters because of their earlier shared-accountability memory. Fox's anger contains fear and frustration, but his narrator should not pretend to know what Gordon feels. Gordon's own viewpoint can show the effort required to stop defending a plan he approved.

>
> Gordon: “I treated a finished investigation as a finished assessment.”
>

>

Neither excuses the other or abandons the partnership. They review the four actual victories and preserved records without pretending those made the Crow beatable. **Return to Blackhaven:** Keep the original shopkeeper and protected contact route central. Fox checks the exit; Gordon asks what help is needed. The shopkeeper may discuss an ordinary difficulty rather than reward the investigators with a speech. Local protection remains meaningful even though the organisation survives.

**Closing:** They commit to specific protection and disruption work. No assured rematch, secret new weakness, full identity reveal or restored fantasy of one decisive victory. Their relationship closes on more honest expectations and continued choice to work together.
