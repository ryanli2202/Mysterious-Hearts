# Style Sheet

The book's house decisions, kept the way a copyeditor keeps them. Read this before drafting or revising prose. `story prose` enforces the lists in the frontmatter: `dialect` (british, american, or unspecified) flags the other dialect's common spellings, each `preferred` entry flags its `avoid` form, `watch-words` are counted in every chapter, and `allow-words` silences a built-in filter word or adverb. Add a `samples` list of your own prose (`../book-one`, approved chapters) and `story prose` compares each chapter with it instead of fixed limits.

## Voice

Preserve the selected route's viewpoint and tense. Follow the shared campaign
revision plan: distinct character voices, concrete consequences, fewer repeated
theme explanations, and no editorial instructions leaking into player prose.
Existing source dialogue is reference material, not blanket approval of every line.

## Spelling And Usage

Preserve each source's spelling until a house dialect is explicitly chosen.
The user's timezone does not establish a spelling preference.

## Capitalisation

Preserve established names, titles, organisation names, powers, and stable IDs.

## Numbers, Dates, And Time

Keep historical dates and ages source-backed. Unknown chronology stays unknown;
never infer a past age or rank from a present-day profile.

## Dialogue And Punctuation

Match the surrounding source and the selected character/era's dialogue guide.

## Character Voices

Read `dialogue_styles.json`, `flashback_character_references.json`, and
`flashback_dialogue_guides.json` in the game root as appropriate. Author separate
era records when work is requested; do not merge present and historical knowledge.

## Watch List

No automated word bans or learned writing preferences have been introduced.
