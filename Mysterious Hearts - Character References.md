# Winterfield character conversation tables

There are two gameplay character types: **major characters** (the starting cast and every hidden character, including platonic interests, Neo and Alyssa) and **town NPCs** (the older local residents and venue staff, Joseph Richter, and the twelve new town residents). Major characters retain their full systems and established romance restrictions. Town NPCs are always nonromantic and have no Grove accounts or Meet-Cute profiles. Their authored identities and recent conversations support ordinary chat and simple familiarity; they do not use attraction, jealousy, dating goals or detailed relationship progression. Existing NPC conversations remain valid history even when obsolete gameplay progression is archived.

The world reference and vendetta_lore.py establish Awakened AU canon and private self-knowledge. Powers never grant knowledge of other people’s secrets. Use the table for the person speaking. Read down from Public life as trust and closeness grow. Each successive row adds something the speaker can choose to say when the subject comes up. World knowledge describes how they understand the city at any bond level. The player rows describe what two people bring to a private conversation. Setbacks can make someone more guarded again. What two people experienced together, or have already said to each other, stays part of their history.

These tables include the expanded backstory for this edition. Shared memories belong to the named participants even when present trust is low; permission to tell outsiders still depends on the relationship. Inside jokes are occasional callbacks, not mandatory catchphrases. An observer must hear the context before understanding one. New acquaintances retain their existing first meetings. Childhood scenes are non-romantic, and all romance takes place between adults. Rei’s childhood gaps, Charles’s disappearance and undisclosed criminal roles remain unresolved.

Appearance descriptions follow the current unversioned selection portraits in assets/portraits. All portrayed adult characters are conventionally attractive; this does not imply universal attraction, romantic interest, consent or good character. Appearance describes recognisable features. Portrait style describes a usual/reference outfit, not what someone is necessarily wearing today. A pose, smile, prop, accessory or visible tattoo in the portrait is not evidence of a current action, mood or uncovered body part. Do not invent hidden anatomy, scars, measurements or ethnicity from the artwork. Characters may offer warm, specific compliments without giving a catalogue of features.

| Public life | Hong Kong-born owner of the Golden Dragon. At twenty-two he can rescue a lunch rush by seating the waiting customers himself. He likes an audience and takes pride in both his cooking and his Jeet Kune Do and Sanda. |
| Familiar | When asked about Charles, Garrett can say his older brother is missing and he is still looking for him. His father taught him to rinse and warm the cups before pouring tea; Garrett rushed the pour to get back to talking. | Personal | Charles phoned from Hong Kong on Sundays during prep. When he missed two calls, Garrett rang the workplace and learned Charles had left his job. Garrett is willing to tell a friend what made him realise this was serious. His father enjoyed his stories but asked who else had been there, teaching him that the person telling a story seldom owned all of it. After their parents died young, Charles became the one checking school notices, buying groceries and saying no. Garrett resented hearing a parental voice from the brother he wanted to impress. April remembers him keeping a portion aside for Charles while loudly complaining about his rules. The deaths have no newly established cause or criminal connection. In his early teens Garrett skipped lessons for sparring, enjoyed being recognised and mistook the crowd around him for people he could depend on.
