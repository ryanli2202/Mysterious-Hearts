# Mysterious Hearts — Winterfield world reference
## Public setting

Winterfield is a US university city with a working waterfront, a downtown arts district and homes in the hills. The playable calendar opens on Saturday, January 1, 2050. People arrange lifts by text, pass around fight clips and still keep paper receipts. A clip can travel halfway round the world before anyone thinks to ask who filmed it. Combat here combines training and Awakened abilities with nerve, fatigue and the possibility of getting hurt. This alternate universe now includes Awakening and rare Traits.

### A day in town

**Mario's Pizza** sits between the Bijou and the bus stop. Mario Romano runs the shop and works the counter himself. He sells square slices from a pan, setting the crisp corner pieces to one side for the regulars who ask for them. On a wet night two people can split a slice at the window and see their bus coming. At the counter a cheese corner is $5, a pepperoni square or a roasted pepper and mushroom slice $6. Mario folds the paper under each slice so friends can carry the whole order to the table.

**Blackstone Headquarters** occupies a converted waterfront freight office. In Winterfield, Blackstone is a private security contractor. Visitors enter from the street to collect a badge, ask about a delivery or speak with Robin Vale at reception. A glass partition looks onto the old loading floor. Beyond it, staff need a pass. Blackstone's presence in town does not establish a connection to a fighter or a particular shipment.

**Interpol Regional Headquarters** houses Winterfield's cross-border liaison staff in the civic district. Dana Lin works the public enquiries desk. A visitor with an overseas document can ask where to send a copy and how to keep the original legible. Local investigators take reports and decide which details need checking abroad.

At **the Golden Dragon**, the lunch rush and the kitchen have different clocks. To a customer the rush ends when a table opens up. To the person at the sink it ends two tubs of bowls later. The noodle shop gives Winterfield a connection to Hong Kong through recipes, family calls and suppliers. A regular can have a strong opinion about the broth and know very little about the owner.

At **Winterfield University**, athletes, artists, medical students and theatre crews all use the campus café. Their versions of the same afternoon sound different. A runner remembers the rain starting on the last lap. Someone carrying a cardboard set model remembers which café door has an awning.

**The municipal boathouse and the Bijou repertory cinema** offer two inexpensive ways to spend an afternoon. At the water, wind dictates whether a beginner gets to steer or stays ashore learning a knot. At the Bijou, patrons study the hand-drawn lobby cards while they wait. A terrible second feature can give two people more to talk about on the way home than a good one.

**East Ward Boxing Club** teaches in daylight. A coach can tell who borrowed a pair of gloves by how tightly the laces come back knotted. The **arena lounge** is a daytime meeting place. The **Iron Circle** runs underground bouts from 20:00. A public sparring session, a grainy clip and a witnessed ring appearance are three different things. Let fighters decide what they disclose about their evenings.

Visitors can try the blind tasting or write a recipe card for the next cook. On **May 11**, **Boathouse Launch Day** opens the municipal dock to people who have never sailed. A friend can remember the ginger in the broth or the sound of the ballot hitting tin because the two of you actually chose that activity.

### When Winterfield makes the news

In a Winterfield conversation, **the Syndicate** means the international criminal organisation. Its name carries the prospect of money, pressure and obligations. The **Red Dragons** are a separate Hong Kong organisation. People also recognise the names **the Conclave** and **Shinokami** from accounts of international crime. A florist, a harbour worker and a crime boss can understand the same joke about joining the Syndicate and have very different things to say in reply.

**Joseph Richter** is Blackstone's wealthy chief executive. Visitors sometimes meet him on his way through the headquarters lobby. Robin Vale can get through three visitor badges while Richter makes one person explain why they have come. He has a habit of looking past the person answering him to see whether the lift has arrived.

In this setting Winterfield police handle local complaints. Interpol is the unified UN government’s global police authority, with cross-border, organised-crime and Awakened jurisdiction. Its regional liaison still helps investigators compare evidence; global authority does not make private files public. The harbour office might ask importers to retain duplicate invoices after a batch of shipping seals turns up twice. The public notice gives the seal numbers, a contact and a deadline. A café customer can read the notice; the private replies go to the investigators. A Red Dragons name in a Hong Kong news clipping can mean quite different things to an old neighbour, a reporter and a detective.

For the wider world, use details people encounter in the course of their own work. A sailor checks a harbour notice before offering a lesson. A cinema patron recognises a sponsor's logo on a programme. A club member compares an edited boxing clip with the round they saw in person. These are scene possibilities. The game clock, scheduled journeys, bookings, discoveries and relationship history determine what has happened. Leave new closures, crimes, named suspects and personal-story clues to authored events.

## Writer-only continuity

**Hamayashi-kai and the Takamuras.** Hamayashi-kai is the yakuza clan that raised Rei and Yuriko. From birth, Rei endured extreme training and torture intended to forge him into a human weapon. At fifteen he had to kill the entire clan to take Yuriko and escape; he then brought them both to the United States. Yuriko was twelve. She was given a much more sheltered life than Rei, but was still subjected to misogynistic ideals, control and abuse. Sheltered does not mean safe or unharmed. Their experiences were unequal, and neither sibling’s suffering cancels the other’s. Rei’s memory gaps remain: the outcome is established, while the exact sequence, methods and escape route are not. This is private family history. Emily and Sophie do not know it from school friendship. Garrett knows only the previously established broad disclosure: a yakuza upbringing, leaving with Yuriko at fifteen and memory gaps. The clan name, extent of torture, Yuriko’s account of abuse and the killings were not part of his disclosure.

**The Joker Cards.** The Cardmaster can call on Ivan Yeun, the Black and White Joker Card, and Sylvanis, the Coloured Joker Card, across all Four Suits. Ivan manages affiliates across Russia and Asia. Sylvanis is the Coloured Joker Card, the Crazed Demon and the Syndicate’s second-in-command: the most feared individual in the world. Theatrical, sadistic and unstable, he seeks chaos and the collapse of social order; fighting is only one amusement. His brilliance makes his cruelty deliberate. His partner-like arrangement with the Cardmaster gives him reach across all Four Suits without making his cooperation dependable. This standing informs his portrayal without giving every resident knowledge of his identity. He keeps the signed order that could expose Ivan. Only the people involved know what has kept Ivan from walking away.

Members meet below the waterfront executive offices. Access requires established Syndicate authorisation. Knowing Sylvanis, befriending him or visiting an affiliate does not grant headquarters entry. The old automatic guest-list mechanic is retired; no recruitment or invitation route currently grants nonmembers access.

**Joseph Richter and Rei Takamura.** After Rei came to Winterfield at fifteen, he and Joseph attended high school together. Joseph and several classmates repeatedly tormented Marcus, one of Rei's closest friends. Marcus later died by suicide. Rei remembers Marcus and Joseph’s part in the bullying. Emily and Sophie know Marcus’s name and that he died by suicide from their shared school years; they do not need the player to explain his death. Both also know from their school years that Joseph and classmates repeatedly bullied Marcus. No specific eyewitness scene is established. No surname, method of death or final message is established. Joseph still speaks of it as something that simply happened at school. Years later, in the Blackstone lobby, he recognises Rei on sight. Rei decides whether to name what Joseph did, talk business or walk away. This history is distinct from Emily's greenhouse incident and from the events Rei cannot remember before his escape. Garrett already knows Marcus’s name, death, Joseph’s repeated bullying and his minimisation from Rei’s private account at the Golden Dragon after closing. Rei first told him the paper-pigeon film story; Garrett listened, was angry at Joseph and asked what Rei needed instead of promising retaliation. Garrett never met Marcus or witnessed the school events. His distrust of Joseph persists, but Joseph does not automatically know that Rei confided in him. Minsu and Blackstone’s staff still need an actual disclosure.

**Charles Han.** Charles is undercover inside the Syndicate. At the opening Garrett knows that the Sunday calls stopped, Charles left work and someone collected his clothes. The first playable evidence comes from Mei, a former dispatch clerk. The correction on a delivery slip resembles Charles's handwriting. Its date falls eleven days after his departure. The receiving copy identifies West Pier Cold Storage in Hong Kong and has no driver's signature. Garrett must inspect each copy through **Find Charles**. The stamp identifies where four crates went. It cannot place Charles at the warehouse. His rank, motive and eventual fate remain open.

**Organisations.** The Red Dragons are Garrett's Hong Kong connection. In Winterfield he runs the Golden Dragon by day and conducts Red Dragons business at their private lounge at night. The Syndicate is a separate organisation. Vendetta's Four Suits offer a vocabulary for planning its work behind the scenes: Diamonds handles finance, Hearts public affairs, Clubs paramilitary operations and Spades assassinations. The Crow, the Aces and the Joker Cards give writers possible figures higher up. Any connection between them and a Winterfield resident needs its own evidence.

**International background.** Vendetta groups the Syndicate, the Conclave and Takeo Mishima's Shinokami under the name *Three Dragon Heads*. Adapt their different sources of influence when planning a future investigation. An old family collection changing hands fits the Conclave's history in banking and antiquities. A sponsor exerting pressure over coverage fits the Syndicate's interest in public reputation. Shinokami's Pacific connections could matter to a cross-border inquiry. None of these examples establishes a Winterfield operation. The unified world government and specialised Interpol Awakened Operations Division also exist in this AU. Source dossier events and individual assessments are not automatically AU history.

**People across the two settings.** Tyler Harding's boxing and care for his father survive in East Ward, where his dad keeps the club books. Valentina Yeun is one of Ivan’s five generals, the Queen of Wind, whose Trait is Deathwind. She also teaches sailing; her independence from her adoptive brother matters both at the boathouse and within his command structure. Daniel Stevens still practises judo, but a double feature of his own choosing matters more to him than proving his pedigree. Their workplaces give the player a way to meet them. Their connections, private addresses and romantic histories follow the save.

### Investigations in the Winterfield continuity

**Interpol liaison.** For a cross-border case, a Winterfield detective keeps the original report and asks a liaison to send a specific question overseas. A Hong Kong colleague might be able to confirm when a company surrendered a warehouse lease. That answer establishes a date. It takes a separate witness to put a person at the building. Jeremy Gordon can serve as a case coordinator in this continuity. Edward Fox is a possible Blackhaven source with old contracting connections. A tip from Fox needs corroboration, and neither man has an established connection to Charles's search.

**The Red Dragons.** In Garrett's old neighbourhood a shopkeeper might remember who paid to replace a broken shutter and also remember feeling unable to refuse the favour. That tension explains why neighbours disagree about the Dragons. A creditor, supplier or former classmate has a particular history with particular people. Knowing Garrett through lunch at the Golden Dragon gives a Winterfield resident no access to the private lounge.

**The Syndicate.** Its branches see different pieces of a job. A Diamonds bookkeeper could trace a payment to a contractor. A Hearts publicist could know who bought space in a festival programme. Each would have to ask somebody else who ordered the work. In a possible later investigation, matching their two records would make a better lead than a stranger who can recite the whole hierarchy. The local festivals stand on their own; their organisers and sponsors have no established criminal ties.

**The Conclave and Shinokami.** The Conclave relies on inherited introductions: a family foundation can lend an object to a gallery while leaving the years between two owners blank. Shinokami's influence runs through Pacific contracting and transport: a dockworker may remember which contractor issued a badge long after a subcontract changed hands. Either pattern can supply a future case. A gap in a catalogue or a familiar shipping company is a reason to ask a question, never proof that a Winterfield resident belongs to either organisation.

**Neo Ishida.** Mishima’s estranged son has made a quiet life repairing electronics in Winterfield. His Shinigami history is coercive combat conditioning rather than hidden cybernetics; Number 13 remains an unresolved private memory, not a spawned antagonist. His best friendships with Tyler and Daniel date to their overlapping high-school years. His shared apartment with Alyssa is private, and Alyssa has her own work at the archive and independent daily routine. His parentage and escape are not public gossip.

**Alyssa.** Neo’s girlfriend is Blisk, with elf-like ears, purple eyes and dark hair. Blisk is a species, not an affiliation. Shared species does not make her related to Sylvanis or part of the Syndicate. Her life in Winterfield centres on bookbinding, botanical sketches, friends and the apartment she shares with Neo.

**Source shelf.** The Vendetta master document and the Garrett Han and Rei Takamura dossiers inform the Hong Kong ties and public lives. *The Iron Circle* informs the fight scene. *The Three Dragon Heads*, *The Crow*, *Syndicate Joker Cards*, the two *Four Aces* dossiers and the Takeo Mishima dossier inform the organisational background. ## Awakening and the unified world · AU canon

The United Nations is the unified world government in 2050. National identities, cultures, cities and local police persist within it. Interpol is its global police authority, not merely a liaison organisation. Its Awakened Operations Division handles assessment, registration, specialist response and investigations of trafficking, coercive recruitment and experimentation. Local reports and the Winterfield public desk remain practical entry points; a badge or a Trait never grants unrestricted records access. Registered, employed and unregistered are different statuses, not moral rankings. No individual registration status is invented merely by importing this institution.

Awakening exceeds ordinary biological limits and may develop through inherited potential or exceptional circumstances. Trauma is neither necessary nor sufficient, and abuse is not a dependable route to power. Most Awakened have enhanced capacities; rare Traits are individual abilities. The source estimates of fewer than 5,000 confirmed Awakened and 150 Trait-users are reference estimates, not an enforced AU census. Winterfield’s unusual concentration is supported by its underground circuit, international criminal connections and specialist Interpol presence. Ordinary people remain essential to community life.

In this AU the term Awakened also includes the explicitly established Blisk characters Alyssa and Sylvanis. They remain Blisk, not human, and neither their powers nor affiliations apply to their entire species. Importing the unified government does not silently import every source war event, family biography or casualty.

### Crime, obligations and ordinary life

The Three Dragon Heads are the Syndicate, the Conclave and Takeo Mishima’s Shinokami. The Red Dragons remain separate. The Syndicate’s Diamonds finance operations, Hearts manage publicity and influence, Clubs provide paramilitary force and Spades conduct assassinations. The Four Aces are mission specialists serving the Crow directly, not Suit leaders; the Joker Cards operate across branches under the Cardmaster, the Crow. His influence lies in money, proxies and compromised institutions, not an obligation to be the strongest fighter. Identities and operations remain compartmentalised.

The Conclave uses old wealth, underground banking, antiquities and inherited introductions. Shinokami uses coercive training, experimentation, armed affiliates and Pacific transport networks. Criminal groups seek Awakened as enforcers, assets and recruits. Their interest creates coercion and competing loyalties, not proof that every Awakened or every sponsored business is criminal. The Red Dragons’ protection and redistribution can help residents while creating debt, pressure and legal consequences. Good intentions do not erase those costs.

Blackstone retains its public private-security business and Joseph Richter’s established history with Rei. Its covert AU remit now includes hunting and coercively recruiting Awakened for mercenary work. This is writer-only institutional canon, not something reception staff, the public or every character automatically knows. No specific abduction, victim, contract, case resolution or connection to Charles has occurred merely because this remit exists. The source Iron Circle rescue, Rei’s capture, Emily’s awakening and the destruction of Blackstone are not imported events.

Valentina is a responsible commander in Ivan’s network, not just a relative shielded from his work. She and Ivan already know each other’s roles. Her generalship does not reveal all branch files, his funding history or Sylvanis’s leverage. She retains her sailing work, home, friendships, familial bond and freedom to choose relationships. Garrett and Valentina’s source romance is not mandatory. No powers change family or consent boundaries.

Playable career decisions now bring these pressures into Red Dragons leadership, Joker Card responsibilities and Iron Circle preparation: community debt, witness privacy, conflicting branch reports, finance and publicity pressure, Awakened recruitment, dangerous bout offers and concealed injuries. Decisions retain the existing craft, reputation, autonomy, trust, fatigue and leadership-pressure effects. Their exact choices and bounded outcomes are saved privately in career history; sharing still requires the existing explicit share action. These are local work decisions, not automatic faction defeats or solved criminal cases. Find Charles retains its evidence sequence and unresolved outcome.

The civilian routines, festivals, home life, creative work and relationships remain independent reasons to play. No source storyline forces Tyler’s father to die, Charles’s fate to resolve, Neo’s home to be destroyed, Ivan to defect or a romance to happen. Emily’s potential Critical Hit requires a separately authored awakening event; none is triggered by dialogue, a bond score or this canon migration.

## Playable everyday continuity · immersion update

Winterfield’s simulated climate has cool winters and warm summers. Weather, daylight and forecasts use the story calendar, never the computer’s real-world date. Rain takes casual outdoor practice beneath the boathouse awning and moves the hidden residents’ exposed leisure activities to the café. A calendar invitation still stands until someone changes it.

In 2050 the electric harbour shuttle uses low-power e-paper departure boards while retaining paper tickets. The university lends repair tools through lockers; the makerspace teaches repairs rather than making practical skills obsolete.
